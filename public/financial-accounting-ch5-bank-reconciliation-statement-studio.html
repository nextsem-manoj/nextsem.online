🏦 1. What is a Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS)?

A Bank Reconciliation Statement is a statement prepared periodically by an account holder to reconcile (tally) the bank balance shown in the business firm's Cash Book (Bank Column) with the balance shown by the bank in the Passbook (Bank Statement) as on a particular date, by setting out the reasons for discrepancies between the two.

🎯 Need for Reconciliation

In theory, the bank balance shown in the Cash Book must agree with the balance shown in the Passbook. However, in practice, these two balances rarely match due to:

  • Time Lag: Delays between the issue/deposit of cheques and their presentation/clearing at the bank.
  • Direct Transactions: Bank deductions, charges, interest, or direct deposits by customers unknown to the firm until the statement arrives.
  • Clerical Errors: Mistakes committed either by the business clerk or by the bank.

🔑 Key Terms Explained

Cash Book (Bank Column)

Maintained by the business firm. Deposits are recorded on the Debit side (Receipts), and withdrawals/payments are recorded on the Credit side (Payments).

Bank Passbook / Statement

A copy of the customer's account in the bank's ledger. Bank credits our account when money is deposited, and debits our account when money is withdrawn.

Favourable Balance

Money is sitting in the bank (Deposits > Withdrawals).
• Cash Book: Debit Balance (Dr.)
• Passbook: Credit Balance (Cr.)

Unfavourable / Bank Overdraft

Business has withdrawn more than deposits (borrowed from bank).
• Cash Book: Credit Balance (Cr.)
• Passbook: Debit Balance (Dr. / OD)

⏳ 5.1.1 Timing Differences (The 7 Time-Lag Causes)

Timing differences arise because of the time gap between recording a transaction in the firm's Cash Book and its actual realization by the bank.

If Starting with Cash Book: ADD (+)

(a) Cheques issued but not yet presented for payment

Firm issues cheque → immediately credited in Cash Book (reducing Cash Book balance). But the payee has not presented it at the bank yet. Passbook balance remains HIGHER.

If Starting with Cash Book: DEDUCT (–)

(b) Cheques paid into bank but not yet collected

Cheques received from customers debited in Cash Book immediately (+). But the clearing house takes a few days. Passbook balance remains LOWER until clearing.

If Starting with Cash Book: DEDUCT (–)

(c) Direct debits / Bank charges by the bank

Bank deducts service charges, collection charges, or interest on overdraft directly. Passbook is debited without firm's immediate knowledge → Passbook balance is LOWER.

If Starting with Cash Book: ADD (+)

(d) Amounts directly deposited by customers in bank

Debtor pays directly into firm's bank account. Bank credits the account immediately, but firm only learns when receiving bank statement → Passbook balance is HIGHER.

If Starting with Cash Book: ADD (+)

(e) Interest and dividends collected by the bank

Bank collects investment dividends or interest and credits the account directly. No entry exists in Cash Book until statement is reviewed → Passbook balance is HIGHER.

If Starting with Cash Book: DEDUCT (–)

(f) Direct payments by bank on standing instructions

Bank automatically pays insurance premium, rent, or telephone bills as per standing instructions. Bank debits account → Passbook balance is LOWER.

If Starting with Cash Book: DEDUCT (–)

(g) Cheques deposited / bills discounted dishonoured

When a deposited cheque or discounted bill bounces, bank debits customer's account immediately. Firm does not know until statement arrives → Passbook balance is LOWER.

⚠️ 5.1.2 Differences Caused by Errors

Errors can occur either on the part of the business firm in the Cash Book or on the part of the bank in the Passbook.

(a) Errors Committed by the Firm in Cash Book

  • Omission of Entry: Cheque deposited or issued completely omitted from Cash Book.
  • Wrong Amount: Cheque of ₹5,000 recorded as ₹500 in Cash Book.
  • Wrong Column: Cheque entered in cash column instead of bank column.
  • Side Reversal: Cheque issued (payment) debited instead of credited.
  • Casting / Carry-forward Errors: Overcasting or undercasting of debit/credit totals, or carrying forward debit balance as credit.

(b) Errors Committed by the Bank in Passbook

  • Wrong Credit: Bank mistakenly credits our account with money deposited by another customer with a similar name.
  • Wrong Debit: Bank mistakenly debits our account for a cheque drawn by another account holder.
  • Duplication: A withdrawal or payment recorded twice in the passbook.
  • Transposition of Figures: ₹1,589 entered as ₹1,598 by the bank operator.

📊 5.2.1 Four-Balance Rule Matrix (Without Adjusting Cash Book)

In the Plus (+) and Minus (–) column method, the golden rule is:

🔑 The Target Book Principle:
If starting with Cash Book, ask: "Did this transaction make the Passbook HIGHER or LOWER than Cash Book?" If higher → ADD (+); if lower → DEDUCT (–).
If starting with Passbook, the treatment is exactly the OPPOSITE!
Discrepancy Item Starting with Cash Book Dr. (Favourable) Starting with Passbook Cr. (Favourable) Starting with Cash Book Cr. (Overdraft) Starting with Passbook Dr. (Overdraft)
Cheques issued but not presented + Plus – Minus + Plus – Minus
Cheques deposited but not cleared – Minus + Plus – Minus + Plus
Direct deposits by customers + Plus – Minus + Plus – Minus
Interest / Dividends collected by bank + Plus – Minus + Plus – Minus
Bank charges / Incidental charges – Minus + Plus – Minus + Plus
Direct payments (Insurance/Rent) by bank – Minus + Plus – Minus + Plus
Dishonour of cheques / discounted bills – Minus + Plus – Minus + Plus
Wrong credit given by bank + Plus – Minus + Plus – Minus
Wrong debit given by bank – Minus + Plus – Minus + Plus

📖 NCERT Questions for Practice: Short & Long Answers

Comprehensive, exam-oriented solutions for all textbook theoretical questions from page 174:

✍️ Part 1: Short Answer Questions

1. State the need for the preparation of bank reconciliation statement? Short Answer

Need for BRS:

  • Finds Missing Discrepancies: Explains why the Cash Book bank balance does not agree with the Passbook balance.
  • Ascertains True Bank Balance: Informs the business of the exact available cash in bank to prevent cheque dishonour.
  • Catches Errors & Frauds: Detects clerical mistakes committed by the cashier or bank and deters embezzlement.
  • Updates Cash Book: Reveals direct bank debits (charges) and credits (customer deposits, interest) unknown to the firm.
2. What is a bank overdraft? Short Answer

Definition: A credit arrangement in which a commercial bank allows a current account holder to withdraw money in excess of the available balance up to an agreed credit limit.

  • Nature: It is a current liability (the business owes money to the bank).
  • In Cash Book: Shown as a Credit Balance (Cr.).
  • In Passbook: Shown as a Debit Balance (Dr.).
3. Briefly explain the statement 'wrongly debited by the bank' with the help of an example. Short Answer

Explanation: An error committed by the bank where it mistakenly deducts (debits) money from a customer's account without any valid cheque, standing order, or authority.

Example: Firm A (Raman Traders) issues a cheque of ₹5,000. The bank clerk erroneously enters the debit into Firm B's account (Raman & Sons) due to similar names. Firm B's passbook is reduced by ₹5,000 wrongly.

Treatment in BRS (Starting with Cash Book): Passbook is lower, so DEDUCT (–) ₹5,000.

4. State the causes of difference occurred due to time lag. Short Answer

Causes Due to Time Lag (Delay in Clearing/Recording):

  1. Cheques issued by the firm but not yet presented for payment by creditors.
  2. Cheques deposited/paid into bank but not yet collected/cleared by the bank.
  3. Bank charges, interest on overdraft, and commission debited directly by the bank.
  4. Interest on investments and dividends collected directly by the bank.
  5. Direct payments into the bank account made by customers (NEFT/RTGS/UPI).
  6. Direct payments by the bank under standing instructions (Insurance, Rent).
  7. Dishonour of a deposited cheque or discounted bill not yet notified to the firm.
5. Briefly explain the term 'favourable balance as per cash book'. Short Answer

Explanation: When total receipts (deposits) in the bank column of the Cash Book exceed total payments (withdrawals), the resulting balance is favourable.

  • Sign: Represented as a Debit Balance (Dr.) in the Cash Book.
  • Asset: It represents the firm's own positive funds sitting in the bank (Current Asset).
  • Passbook Counterpart: Corresponds to a Credit Balance (Cr.) in the Bank Passbook.
6. Enumerate the steps to ascertain the correct cash book balance. Short Answer

Steps to Ascertain Correct (Amended) Cash Book Balance:

  1. Take the unadjusted closing balance of the Cash Book.
  2. Debit Side: Enter transactions credited in passbook only (direct customer deposits, interest, dividends collected, rectification of errors that understated cash).
  3. Credit Side: Enter transactions debited in passbook only (bank charges, standing order auto-debits, dishonoured cheques, rectification of errors that overstated cash).
  4. Exclude Timing Differences: Never enter unpresented or uncollected cheques in the amended cash book.
  5. Balance the Cash Book to find the Adjusted Cash Book Balance for the Balance Sheet and BRS.

📝 Part 2: Long Answer Questions

1. What is a bank reconciliation statement? Why is it prepared? Long Answer

Definition: A Bank Reconciliation Statement (BRS) is a periodic explanatory statement prepared by an account holder to reconcile, explain, and tally the causes of differences between the bank balance shown in the Cash Book (bank column) and the Bank Passbook as on a specific date.

Detailed Objectives of Preparation:

  • 1. Identification of Discrepancies: Explains the exact rupee difference between the firm's ledger and the bank's records.
  • 2. Determination of Real Bank Balance: Prevents cheques from bouncing by ensuring the firm knows the exact cleared balance.
  • 3. Rectification of Clerical Errors: Discloses casting mistakes, transposition of digits, or omissions in both records.
  • 4. Fraud Prevention & Internal Check: Uncovers delays in depositing cash or cheques collected by sales staff.
  • 5. Ledger Updating: Facilitates recording of direct customer deposits, bank fees, and interest into the official accounts.
  • 6. Audit Verification: Serves as authoritative evidence for statutory auditors verifying cash and bank balances.
2. Explain the reasons where the balance shown by the bank passbook does not agree with the cash book. Long Answer

The causes fall into two primary categories:

A. Differences Caused by Time Lag:

  • Cheques issued but unpresented: Deducted in Cash Book; Passbook is Higher.
  • Cheques deposited but uncollected: Added in Cash Book; Passbook is Lower.
  • Direct bank charges & interest on overdraft: Debited by bank; Passbook is Lower.
  • Direct deposits by customers: Credited by bank; Passbook is Higher.
  • Dividends & interest collected by bank: Credited by bank; Passbook is Higher.
  • Direct payments on standing instructions: Debited by bank; Passbook is Lower.
  • Dishonour of cheques/bills: Debited by bank; Passbook is Lower.

B. Differences Caused by Errors:

  • Errors by the firm: Undercasting or overcasting of totals, posting in wrong columns, or omissions.
  • Errors by the bank: Wrong debit/credit to customer's account, transposition of digits, or duplicate entries.
3. Explain the process of preparing bank reconciliation statement with amended cash balance. Long Answer

Two-Stage Reconciliation Process:

Stage 1: Preparation of Amended Cash Book:

  • Open Cash Book with the existing unadjusted closing balance.
  • Debit Side (+): Direct customer collections, interest/dividends collected, rectification of errors that understated cash.
  • Credit Side (–): Bank charges, standing orders paid, dishonoured cheques, rectification of errors that overstated cash.
  • Exclude Timing Differences: Never enter unpresented or uncollected cheques.
  • Balance the Cash Book to arrive at the Adjusted Cash Book Balance.

Stage 2: Preparation of BRS:

  • Start with the Adjusted Cash Book Balance.
  • Add: Cheques issued but not yet presented for payment (+ bank erroneous credits).
  • Less: Cheques deposited but not yet collected by bank (+ bank erroneous debits).
  • Result = Balance as per Bank Passbook!

Q5: Naman Cash Book Balance ₹40,960 (March 31) Ans: Passbook ₹50,870
