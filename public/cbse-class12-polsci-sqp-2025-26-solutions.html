The end of bipolarity in international politics led to an increase in ___________.
1 Mark
A) Proxy wars between super powers
B) Arms race between super powers
✓ C) Multilateral diplomacy and cooperation
D) Bipolarity in international politics
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
Answer & Explanation
The collapse of the Soviet Union (1991) ended the bipolar world order. With the US and USSR no longer competing as two blocs, international politics moved toward multilateralism. Countries began cooperating through multilateral institutions like the UN, WTO, and IMF rather than forming military blocs.
💡
Exam HintKeyword: "End of Cold War → Rise of Multilateralism". Chapter 1 — The End of Bipolarity. Remember the year 1991.
उत्तर एवं व्याख्या
सोवियत संघ के विघटन (1991) के बाद द्विध्रुवीय विश्व व्यवस्था समाप्त हो गई। इसके बाद अंतर्राष्ट्रीय राजनीति में बहुपक्षीय कूटनीति और सहयोग में वृद्धि हुई। देश अब सैन्य गुटों के बजाय संयुक्त राष्ट्र, WTO जैसी बहुपक्षीय संस्थाओं के माध्यम से सहयोग करने लगे।
💡
परीक्षा संकेतयाद रखें: "शीत युद्ध की समाप्ति → बहुपक्षवाद का उदय"। अध्याय 1 — द्विध्रुवीयता का अंत।

1 Mark
✓ A) Rio Summit
B) Antarctic Treaty
C) Montreal Protocol
D) Antarctic Environmental Protocol
✓ Correct Answer: (A)
Answer & Explanation
Agenda-21 was adopted at the Rio Earth Summit (1992) — officially the United Nations Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED) held in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. It is a non-binding action plan for sustainable development linking economic growth with environmental protection.
  • Montreal Protocol (1987) — Ozone layer protection
  • Antarctic Treaty (1959) — Peaceful use of Antarctica

Statement I: In the traditional view of security, most threats to a country's security come from outside its borders.
Statement II: In world politics, each country has to be responsible for its own security.

In the light of above statements, choose the correct answer:
1 Mark
A) Only Statement I is correct
B) Only Statement II is correct
✓ C) Both Statements I and II are correct
D) Both Statements I and II are not correct
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
Answer & Explanation
Statement I is TRUE: The traditional view of security focuses on military threats from other states. The main concern is protecting borders from external aggression.

Statement II is TRUE: In the traditional view, since there is no world government above nation-states, each country must ensure its own security through military power, alliances, or balance of power.
💡

कथन II सत्य है: राष्ट्र-राज्यों के ऊपर कोई विश्व सरकार नहीं होती, इसलिए प्रत्येक देश को अपनी सुरक्षा स्वयं करनी होती है — सैन्य शक्ति, गठबंधन या शक्ति-संतुलन के माध्यम से।

The idea of global security emerged in:
1 Mark
A) 1980s
B) 1970s
C) 1980s
✓ D) 1990s
✓ Correct Answer: (D) — 1990s
Answer & Explanation
The concept of global/cooperative security emerged in the 1990s after the end of the Cold War. With bipolarity gone, countries recognized that threats like terrorism, nuclear proliferation, climate change, and pandemics required collective global responses rather than individual state action.

Assertion (A): At the G-8 meeting in June 2005, India pointed out that the per capita emission rates of the developing countries are a tiny fraction of those in the developed world.
Reason (R): A review of the implementation of the agreements at the Earth Summit in Rio was undertaken by India in 1997.

Choose the appropriate option:
1 Mark
A) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A
B) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A
✓ C) A is true, but R is false
D) A is false, but R is true
✓ Correct Answer: (C)
Answer & Explanation
Assertion (A) is TRUE: India has consistently argued that developing nations have far lower per capita emissions compared to industrialized countries. India raised this at the G-8 Gleneagles Summit, 2005.

Reason (R) is FALSE: The review of Rio Earth Summit agreements (Earth Summit+5) was held as a UN General Assembly Special Session in New York in 1997 — it was a global review, not undertaken by India alone.
💡
Exam HintA = Correct fact about India's position. R = Incorrect — it was a UN review (Earth Summit+5), not by India. Chapter 8.
उत्तर एवं व्याख्या

कारण (R) असत्य है: रियो पृथ्वी सम्मेलन की समीक्षा (Earth Summit+5) 1997 में न्यूयॉर्क में संयुक्त राष्ट्र महासभा के विशेष सत्र में हुई थी — यह वैश्विक समीक्षा थी, केवल भारत द्वारा नहीं।

1 Mark
A) Pursuing Territorial expansion
B) Promoting Economic exploitation
✓ C) National security and economic development

What were the early initiatives taken by the Planning Commission (now NITI Aayog) for building a new India?
2 Marks
Model Answer (2 Marks)
The Planning Commission was established in 1950 under PM Nehru. Its early initiatives included:
  • Five Year Plans: The First Five Year Plan (1951-56) focused on agriculture and irrigation to address food scarcity
  • Mixed Economy Model: Combined public sector investment in heavy industries with private enterprise, balancing growth and equity
  • Land Reforms: Recommended abolition of zamindari system and equitable redistribution of resources
  • Industrial Policy: Guided development of large-scale public sector industries (steel, power, defense)
💡
Key PointsPlanning Commission (1950) → Five Year Plans → Mixed Economy → Land Reforms → Public sector. Chapter 3 (Part B) — Politics of Planned Development.
आदर्श उत्तर (2 अंक)
योजना आयोग की स्थापना 1950 में PM नेहरू के नेतृत्व में हुई। इसकी प्रारंभिक पहलें:
  • पंचवर्षीय योजनाएँ: प्रथम पंचवर्षीय योजना (1951-56) कृषि और सिंचाई पर केन्द्रित थी
  • मिश्रित अर्थव्यवस्था: सार्वजनिक क्षेत्र और निजी उद्यम का संयोजन
  • भूमि सुधार: जमींदारी उन्मूलन और संसाधनों का समान वितरण
  • औद्योगिक नीति: इस्पात, विद्युत, रक्षा जैसे भारी उद्योगों का विकास

मुख्य बिंदुयोजना आयोग → पंचवर्षीय योजनाएँ → मिश्रित अर्थव्यवस्था → भूमि सुधार। अध्याय 3 (भाग B)।

What do you understand by the New International Economic Order (NIEO)? Why did developing countries demand it? Explain.
6 Marks
Model Answer (6 Marks)
New International Economic Order (NIEO):
The NIEO was a set of proposals put forward by developing nations in the 1970s through the UN to restructure the global economic system in their favor. It was formally articulated in the UN General Assembly Declaration (1974).

Background:
After decolonization, former colonies found themselves economically dependent on their former colonial masters. The existing international economic order (Bretton Woods — World Bank, IMF, GATT) was seen as designed to benefit rich nations.

Key Demands of NIEO:
  • 1. Sovereignty over Natural Resources: Developing nations demanded the right to nationalize foreign companies and control their own natural resources
  • 2. Fair Trade Prices: Better and stable prices for their commodity exports (commodities vs. manufactured goods price gap — "terms of trade" problem)
  • 3. Technology Transfer: Access to modern technology from developed nations at affordable costs
  • 4. Debt Relief: Reduction of debt burden and better loan terms from international financial institutions
  • 5. More Representation: Greater voice in decision-making of World Bank and IMF
  • 6. Preferential Market Access: Developed nations should open markets to developing country exports

Why Developing Nations Demanded:
The existing system maintained the colonial economic structure — raw material exporters vs. industrial goods importers. The Group of 77 (G-77) developing nations united to demand systemic reform. The UNCTAD (UN Conference on Trade and Development) became their platform.

Outcome: NIEO demands were largely not implemented as Western nations resisted. However, it raised awareness of global economic inequality and influenced later WTO negotiations and debt relief initiatives.

India's Nuclear Policy:

Historical Background:
  • 1974: India's first nuclear test — "Pokhran I" (Operation Smiling Buddha). India declared it a "peaceful nuclear explosion".

Main Features of India's Nuclear Doctrine:
  • 1. No First Use (NFU): India will not be the first to use nuclear weapons. Nuclear weapons are for deterrence only, not war-fighting.

  • 5. Opposition to NPT (Non-Proliferation Treaty): India has not signed the NPT as it considers it discriminatory — it allows the "P-5" nations to keep nukes while others cannot develop them.
  • 6. Support for Global Disarmament: India supports universal nuclear disarmament through CTBT and other multilateral frameworks.

दक्षिण एशिया में लोकतंत्र — अवलोकन:

दक्षिण एशिया 1.5 अरब की आबादी का घर है और 8 राष्ट्रों से मिलकर बना है।

लोकतंत्र को बनाए रखने वाले देश:
  • भारत: 1947 से अखंड लोकतंत्र (1975-77 आपातकाल को छोड़कर)। विश्व का सबसे बड़ा लोकतंत्र। मजबूत संस्थाएँ, स्वतंत्र न्यायपालिका।
  • श्रीलंका: नियमित चुनाव, हालाँकि जातीय संघर्ष (तमिल मुद्दा) ने अस्थिरता पैदा की।
  • मालदीव: 2008 में लोकतंत्र में परिवर्तन।

लोकतंत्र बनाए न रख सकने वाले देश:
  • पाकिस्तान: बार-बार सैन्य तख्तापलट (1958, 1969, 1977, 1999)। सेना का दबदबा।
  • बांग्लादेश: 1971 की स्वतंत्रता के बाद सैन्य शासन। 1990 के दशक में लोकतंत्र की वापसी।
  • नेपाल: राजतंत्र बनाम लोकतंत्र का संघर्ष — 2008 में गणराज्य बना।
  • म्याँमार: सैन्य जुंटा का शासन; 2021 में तख्तापलट।

चुनौतियाँ: शक्तिशाली सेना + जातीय विभाजन + गरीबी + बाह्य हस्तक्षेप + कमजोर संस्थाएँ।
