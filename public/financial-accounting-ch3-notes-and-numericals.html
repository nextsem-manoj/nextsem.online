Source Documents, Accounting Vouchers & Transaction Evidence

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Source Documents: The primary written evidence supporting an accounting entry (Cash Memo for cash sales/purchases, Invoice/Bill for credit sales/purchases, Receipt for cash received, Pay-in-slip for bank deposits, Cheque for bank payments, Debit Note for returns to suppliers, Credit Note for returns from customers).
  • Accounting Vouchers: Documents prepared by the accountant to authorize recording in books of original entry:
    • Debit Voucher (Payment Voucher): Prepared when cash or bank payments are made.
    • Credit Voucher (Receipt Voucher): Prepared when cash or cheques are received.
    • Transfer Voucher (Non-Cash Voucher / Journal Voucher): Prepared for non-cash transactions (credit purchases, depreciation, bad debts, returns).
    • Complex / Compound Voucher: Involves multiple debits and multiple credits.
✏️ Advanced Conceptual Calculation Problems (Strict Mode — No Hints) 4 Problems

👁️ Check Solution₹10,800 [₹12,000 - 10% Trade Discount (₹1,200) = ₹10,800]

2. A firm receives 3 cash payments on the same day: ₹18,000 from Rohit, ₹24,000 cash sales, and ₹6,000 interest. The total amount authorized on the credit cash vouchers for the day is (2).
👁️ Check Solution₹48,000 [₹18,000 + ₹24,000 + ₹6,000 = ₹48,000]

3. When goods worth ₹50,000 are sold for ₹65,000 on credit, the source document issued to the buyer is a (3) showing an invoice amount of ₹65,000.

The Fundamental Accounting Equation ($A = L + C$)

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Dual Aspect Principle: Every business transaction exerts an equal and simultaneous effect on at least two accounts.
  • The Balance Sheet Equation: \text{Total Assets } (A) = \text{External Liabilities } (L) + \text{Capital / Owner's Equity } (C)
  • Transaction Net Impact on Capital:
    • Revenues & Gains increase Capital (+$R$).
    • Expenses & Losses decrease Capital (-$E$).
    • Fresh Capital introduced increases Capital (+$C_{\text{fresh}}$).
    • Drawings (cash or goods at cost) decrease Capital (-$D$).
✏️ Advanced Conceptual Calculation Problems (Strict Mode — No Hints) 4 Problems

1. A business commenced with Cash ₹1,50,000, Bank Balance ₹2,50,000, Stock ₹1,00,000, and Machinery ₹80,000. It took a loan of ₹1,80,000 from HDFC Bank. The proprietor's initial Capital is (1).

👁️ Check Solution₹4,00,000 [Assets: 1,50,000 + 2,50,000 + 1,00,000 + 80,000 = ₹5,80,000; Capital = Assets - Loan (5,80,000 - 1,80,000) = ₹4,00,000]

2. Goods costing ₹45,000 are sold for ₹60,000. The buyer pays 40% in cash and the remaining 60% on credit. The net increase in Capital resulting from this transaction is (2).
👁️ Check Solution₹15,000 [Selling Price (₹60,000) - Cost Price (₹45,000) = ₹15,000 Profit increases Capital]

3. Total assets of a business are ₹14,00,000. External liabilities are 30% of total assets. The owner's capital is (3).
👁️ Check Solution₹9,80,000 [Liabilities = 30% of 14,00,000 = ₹4,20,000; Capital = 14,00,000 - 4,20,000 = ₹9,80,000]

4. Proprietor withdrew ₹20,000 cash and goods costing ₹15,000 (sale price ₹22,000) for personal use. Total drawings deducted from Capital equal (4).
👁️ Check Solution₹35,000 [Cash (₹20,000) + Goods at Cost (₹15,000) = ₹35,000]

Advanced Capital Algebra: Opening & Closing Balances and Profit

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Master Capital Formula: C_{\text{Closing}} = C_{\text{Opening}} + C_{\text{Additional}} + \text{Net Profit} - \text{Drawings}
  • Derived Net Profit Formula: \text{Net Profit} = C_{\text{Closing}} + \text{Drawings} - C_{\text{Additional}} - C_{\text{Opening}}
  • Balance Sheet Closing Capital: C_{\text{Closing}} = \text{Total Assets}_{\text{Closing}} - \text{Total Liabilities}_{\text{Closing}}
✏️ Advanced Conceptual Calculation Problems (Strict Mode — No Hints) 4 Problems

1. On 31st March 2026, a firm's Total Assets are ₹9,60,000 and Liabilities are ₹3,40,000. If Opening Capital on 1st April 2025 was ₹4,50,000, Additional Capital introduced was ₹70,000, and Drawings were ₹40,000, the Net Profit earned during the financial year is (1).

👁️ Check Solution₹1,40,000 [Closing Capital = 9,60,000 - 3,40,000 = ₹6,20,000; Profit = 6,20,000 + 40,000 - 70,000 - 4,50,000 = ₹1,40,000]

2. A business sustained a Net Loss of ₹65,000 during the year. If Closing Capital is ₹4,20,000, Drawings are ₹55,000, and Additional Capital is ₹40,000, the Opening Capital was (2).
👁️ Check Solution₹5,00,000 [Opening Capital = Closing Capital (4,20,000) + Drawings (55,000) + Net Loss (65,000) - Additional Capital (40,000) = ₹5,00,000]

3. Total Assets of a trader are ₹18,00,000. Capital is 4 times the External Liabilities. The value of External Liabilities is (3).
👁️ Check Solution₹3,60,000 [L + 4L = 18,00,000 ⇒ 5L = 18,00,000 ⇒ L = ₹3,60,000]

4. In the previous problem, the amount of Capital in the business is (4).
👁️ Check Solution₹14,40,000 [4 × 3,60,000 = ₹14,40,000]

Modern Rules of Debit and Credit (Classification of Accounts)

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Modern 5-Category Rule Matrix:
    • Assets: Increase $\rightarrow$ Debit (Dr), Decrease $\rightarrow$ Credit (Cr).
    • Liabilities: Increase $\rightarrow$ Credit (Cr), Decrease $\rightarrow$ Debit (Dr).
    • Capital: Increase $\rightarrow$ Credit (Cr), Decrease $\rightarrow$ Debit (Dr).
    • Expenses / Losses: Increase $\rightarrow$ Debit (Dr), Decrease $\rightarrow$ Credit (Cr).
    • Revenues / Gains: Increase $\rightarrow$ Credit (Cr), Decrease $\rightarrow$ Debit (Dr).
  • Traditional 3-Category Golden Rules:
    • Personal Accounts: Debit the Receiver, Credit the Giver.
    • Real Accounts: Debit what comes in, Credit what goes out.
    • Nominal Accounts: Debit all expenses and losses, Credit all incomes and gains.

Composite Trade & Cash Discount Calculations (Journal Entries)

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Trade Discount: Allowed by wholesaler/manufacturer on catalogue list price. Never recorded in ledger or journal books; only net invoice price is recorded.
  • Cash Discount: Allowed for prompt/immediate payment within credit terms. Always recorded in books (Discount Allowed Dr for seller / Discount Received Cr for buyer).
  • Rule for Partial Payments: Cash discount is calculated ONLY on the cash/cheque portion actually paid, never on the credit portion!
✏️ Advanced Conceptual Calculation Problems (Strict Mode — No Hints) 4 Problems

👁️ Check Solution₹2,880 [Net Invoice = 1,20,000 - 20% (24,000) = ₹96,000; 60% Cash Portion = 0.60 × 96,000 = ₹57,600; Cash Discount = 5% of 57,600 = ₹2,880]

2. In the previous transaction, the actual net cash received from the customer is (2).
👁️ Check Solution₹54,720 [₹57,600 - ₹2,880 = ₹54,720]

3. In the same transaction, the amount debited to the customer's personal debtor account for the remaining 40% credit balance is (3).
👁️ Check Solution₹38,400 [40% of ₹96,000 = ₹38,400]

4. Bought goods from Vikas for catalogue price ₹2,00,000 at 15% trade discount and 3% cash discount. Paid 50% immediately by cheque. The total amount debited to Purchases Account is (4).
👁️ Check Solution₹1,70,000 [Purchases A/c is debited with net invoice price: 2,00,000 - 15% (30,000) = ₹1,70,000]

Capital Expenditure on Assets & Compound Journal Entries

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Capitalization of Asset Acquisition Costs: All direct expenses incurred to bring an asset to working condition (freight, carriage inward, transit insurance, import duty, overhaul of second-hand machinery, installation wages, legal fees for title deed) are debited directly to the Asset Account, not to expense accounts!
  • Compound Journal Entry: A single entry combining two or more accounts on the debit side or credit side for simultaneous related transactions.

Bad Debts, Insolvency Recovery & Accidental Loss Settlements

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Bad Debts: An irrecoverable debt from a debtor due to bankruptcy or insolvency. Debited to Bad Debts A/c (Nominal Loss) and credited to the Debtor's Personal A/c.
  • Recovery of Bad Debts: When an amount previously written off as bad debt is later recovered, it is credited to Bad Debts Recovered A/c (Nominal Gain), NEVER to the debtor's personal account!
  • Goods Lost by Fire / Theft:
    • Total Cost of Lost Goods $\rightarrow$ Credited to Purchases A/c.
    • Insurance Claim Admitted $\rightarrow$ Debited to Insurance Company (Asset).
    • Uninsured Loss $\rightarrow$ Debited to Loss by Fire / Profit & Loss A/c.
✏️ Advanced Conceptual Calculation Problems (Strict Mode — No Hints) 4 Problems

1. Debtor Harish owed ₹60,000. He became bankrupt and his estate pays an official dividend of 45 paise in a rupee. The amount written off as Bad Debts is (1).

👁️ Check Solution₹33,000 [Cash Recovered = 45% of 60,000 = ₹27,000; Bad Debts Loss = 55% of 60,000 = ₹33,000]

2. An amount of ₹18,000 written off as bad debt two years ago was unexpectedly recovered to the extent of 80% via bank draft. The amount credited to Bad Debts Recovered A/c is (2).
👁️ Check Solution₹14,400 [80% of ₹18,000 = ₹14,400]

3. Goods costing ₹75,000 were destroyed by an accidental fire. The insurance surveyor inspected the site and admitted a formal claim for 70%. The net amount debited to Loss by Fire Account in P&L is (3).
👁️ Check Solution₹22,500 [Total Loss (₹75,000) - Insurance Claim Admitted (₹52,500) = ₹22,500 Uninsured Loss]

4. In the previous fire insurance settlement, the total amount credited to Purchases Account is (4).
👁️ Check Solution₹75,000 [Purchases A/c is credited with full cost value of goods destroyed]

Banking Operations, Overdraft Charges & Personal Drawings

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Distinction between Business Expense and Drawings:
    • Life Insurance Premium of Proprietor $\rightarrow$ Debited to Drawings A/c.
    • Income Tax of Sole Proprietor $\rightarrow$ Debited to Drawings A/c (as income tax is a personal liability).
    • Fire/Theft Insurance of Goods/Building $\rightarrow$ Debited to Insurance Expense A/c.
    • GST / Business Municipal Taxes $\rightarrow$ Debited to Rates & Taxes / GST Expense A/c.
  • Bank Deductions: Bank charges and overdraft interest reduce the bank balance immediately and are debited to Bank Charges / Interest A/c.

Ledger Posting Mechanics, Folio Cross-References & T-Accounts

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • The Ledger: The principal book of accounts containing all personal, real, and nominal accounts.
  • Posting Rules:
    • The account debited in the journal is debited in the ledger with the prefix "To [Credit Account Name]".
    • The account credited in the journal is credited in the ledger with the prefix "By [Debit Account Name]".
  • Cross-Referencing: Journal contains Ledger Folio (L.F.) column; Ledger contains Journal Folio (J.F.) column to facilitate audit tracing.

Ledger Balancing Arithmetic & Extracting Trial Balances

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Balancing Mechanics:
    • If $\text{Debit Total} > \text{Credit Total}$, difference is inserted on the credit side as "By Balance c/d" (represents a Debit Balance brought down on debit side as "To Balance b/d").
    • If $\text{Credit Total} > \text{Debit Total}$, difference is inserted on the debit side as "To Balance c/d" (represents a Credit Balance brought down on credit side as "By Balance b/d").
  • Normal Account Balances:
    • Assets & Expenses $\rightarrow$ Always carry Debit Balances.
    • Liabilities, Capital & Incomes $\rightarrow$ Always carry Credit Balances.
