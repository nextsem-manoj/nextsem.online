Electromagnetic Induction (EMI)

Mastery Score: 0 / 10 Mastered

1. Magnetic Flux ($\Phi_B$)

Magnetic flux through a plane surface of area $A$ in a uniform magnetic field $B$ measures the total number of magnetic lines of force passing through it:

$$\Phi_B = \vec{B} \cdot \vec{A} = B A \cos\theta$$
  • $\theta$: Angle between magnetic field vector $\vec{B}$ and the outward normal area vector $\vec{A}$.
  • SI Unit: Weber ($\text{Wb}$) or $\text{T}\cdot\text{m}^2$. Dimensions: $[M L^2 T^{-2} A^{-1}]$.
  • Scalar Quantity: It can be positive, negative, or zero depending on $\theta$.

2. Faraday’s Laws & Induced Current / Charge

First Law: Emf is induced whenever the magnetic flux linked with a circuit changes with time.

Second Law: The magnitude of induced emf is equal to the time rate of change of magnetic flux:

$$\varepsilon = -N \frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}$$ $$I = \frac{|\varepsilon|}{R} = \frac{N}{R} \left|\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}\right|, \quad \Delta q = \frac{N \Delta \Phi_B}{R}$$

Crucial Insight: Induced electric charge $\Delta q$ depends strictly on the net change in flux $\Delta \Phi_B$ and resistance $R$, and is independent of time duration!

3. Lenz’s Law and Conservation of Energy

The polarity of induced emf produces an induced current whose magnetic effect opposes the change in magnetic flux that causes it.

$$\text{Work done against magnetic opposition} = \text{Electrical Energy} = \text{Joule Heat } (I^2 R t)$$

4. Motional Electromotive Force

(a) Linear Sliding Rod ($l$ moving with velocity $v$ in field $B$):

$$\varepsilon = B l v, \quad I = \frac{Blv}{R}, \quad F_{\text{ext}} = \frac{B^2 l^2 v}{R}, \quad P_{\text{mech}} = \frac{B^2 l^2 v^2}{R} = I^2 R$$

(b) Rotational Rod / Spoked Wheel (Radius $R$ rotating at $\omega$ rad/s):

$$\varepsilon = \frac{1}{2} B \omega R^2 = \pi \nu B R^2$$

5. Inductance (Mutual & Self) & Magnetic Energy

$$\text{Self-Inductance: } N\Phi_B = L I \implies \varepsilon = -L \frac{dI}{dt}$$ $$\text{Long Solenoid: } L = \mu_0 \mu_r n^2 A l = \frac{\mu_0 \mu_r N^2 A}{l}$$ $$\text{Mutual Inductance: } M = \mu_0 \mu_r n_1 n_2 \pi r_1^2 l = \frac{\mu_0 \mu_r N_1 N_2 A}{l}$$ $$\text{Stored Energy: } U_B = \frac{1}{2} L I^2, \quad \text{Energy Density: } u_B = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$$

6. AC Generator

Converts mechanical rotation into sinusoidal alternating emf:

$$\varepsilon(t) = N B A \omega \sin(\omega t) = \varepsilon_0 \sin(2\pi \nu t)$$ $$\text{Peak Voltage: } \varepsilon_0 = N B A \omega, \quad V_{\text{rms}} = \frac{\varepsilon_0}{\sqrt{2}}$$

Numerical 1 Topic 1: Magnetic Flux in 3D
A square coil of side $20\text{ cm}$ with $50\text{ turns}$ is in the $X\text{-}Y$ plane in a magnetic field $\vec{B} = (0.2\hat{i} + 0.3\hat{j} + 0.4\hat{k})\text{ T}$. Calculate the total magnetic flux linked with the coil in Webers.
Area vector $\vec{A} = (0.20\text{ m})^2\hat{k} = 0.04\hat{k}\text{ m}^2$.
Flux per turn $\Phi_1 = \vec{B}\cdot\vec{A} = 0.4 \times 0.04 = 0.016\text{ Wb}$.
Total flux $\Phi_{\text{total}} = N \Phi_1 = 50 \times 0.016 = \mathbf{0.80\text{ Wb}}$.
Numerical 2 Topic 2: Faraday's Law & Total Charge
A coil of $500\text{ turns}$ and resistance $4\,\Omega$ has area $0.01\pi\text{ m}^2$. It is in a normal field $B = 0.2\text{ T}$ and rotated $180^\circ$ in $0.1\text{ s}$. Find the total induced charge $\Delta q$ that flows in Coulombs (take $\pi = 3.1416$).
$\Delta \Phi = \Phi_2 - \Phi_1 = -BA - BA = -2BA = -2(0.2)(0.01\pi) = -0.004\pi\text{ Wb}$.
$\Delta q = \frac{N |\Delta \Phi|}{R} = \frac{500 \times 0.004\pi}{4} = 0.5\pi \approx \mathbf{1.57\text{ C}}$.
Numerical 3 Topic 2: Solenoid Loop & Rate of Change
A solenoid with $1000\text{ turns/m}$ carries current $I(t) = 3t^2 + 2t + 1\text{ A}$. A loop of area $4 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2$ is placed inside. Calculate the induced emf at $t = 2\text{ s}$ in microvolts ($\mu\text{V}$). (Use $\pi = 3.1416$).
At $t = 2\text{ s}$, $\frac{dI}{dt} = 6(2)+2 = 14\text{ A/s}$.
$\frac{dB}{dt} = \mu_0 n \frac{dI}{dt} = (4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 1000 \times 14 = 5.6\pi \times 10^{-3}\text{ T/s}$.
$\varepsilon = A \frac{dB}{dt} = (4 \times 10^{-4}) \times (5.6\pi \times 10^{-3}) = 2.24\pi \times 10^{-6}\text{ V} \approx \mathbf{7.04\,\mu\text{V}}$.
Numerical 4 Topic 4: Rectangular Loop Exiting Field
A rectangular loop $12\text{ cm} \times 4\text{ cm}$ moves at $2\text{ cm/s}$ out of a normal field $B = 0.5\text{ T}$ parallel to its shorter side (cutting length = $12\text{ cm}$). Find the induced emf in millivolts ($\text{mV}$).
$\varepsilon = B l v = 0.5\text{ T} \times 0.12\text{ m} \times 0.02\text{ m/s} = 0.0012\text{ V} = \mathbf{1.2\text{ mV}}$.
Numerical 5 Topic 4: Motional EMF & Mechanical Power
A rod of length $l = 0.5\text{ m}$ slides on rails at $v = 4\text{ m/s}$ in $B = 1.2\text{ T}$ across a resistor $R = 2\,\Omega$. Calculate the mechanical power supplied in Watts.
$\varepsilon = Blv = 1.2 \times 0.5 \times 4 = 2.4\text{ V}$.
$I = \varepsilon / R = 2.4 / 2 = 1.2\text{ A}$.
$P = I^2 R = (1.2)^2 \times 2 = \mathbf{2.88\text{ W}}$.
Numerical 6 Topic 4: Rotational Motional EMF
A wheel with spokes of length $0.8\text{ m}$ rotates at $180\text{ rpm}$ in a normal field $B = 0.4 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$. Calculate the induced emf in millivolts ($\text{mV}$). (Use $\pi = 3.1416$).
$\omega = 2\pi \times (180/60) = 6\pi\text{ rad/s}$.
$\varepsilon = \frac{1}{2} B \omega R^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times (0.4 \times 10^{-4}) \times (6\pi) \times (0.64) = 0.768\pi \times 10^{-4}\text{ V} \approx \mathbf{0.241\text{ mV}}$.
Numerical 7 Topic 5: Solenoid Self-Inductance
A solenoid of length $0.5\text{ m}$, area $25\text{ cm}^2$, and $800\text{ turns}$ has an air core. If the current drops from $4.0\text{ A}$ to $0$ in $2.0\text{ ms}$, calculate the back-emf induced in Volts. (Use $\pi = 3.1416$).
$L = \frac{\mu_0 N^2 A}{l} = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 640000 \times 2.5 \times 10^{-3}}{0.5} = 4.02\text{ mH}$.
$\varepsilon = L \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t} = (4.02 \times 10^{-3}) \times 2000 = \mathbf{8.04\text{ V}}$.
Numerical 8 Topic 5: Magnetic Stored Energy
A superconducting solenoid of inductance $L = 5.0\text{ H}$ carries a current of $60\text{ A}$. Calculate the total magnetic energy stored in kilojoules ($\text{kJ}$).
$U_B = \frac{1}{2} L I^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 5.0 \times (60)^2 = 2.5 \times 3600 = 9000\text{ J} = \mathbf{9.0\text{ kJ}}$.
Numerical 9 Topic 5: Mutual Inductance
Two coaxial solenoids of length $1.0\text{ m}$ have $N_1 = 600, N_2 = 1000$ turns and inner radius $r_1 = 2\text{ cm}$. Find their mutual inductance $M$ in millihenries ($\text{mH}$). (Use $\pi^2 \approx 9.87$).
$M = \frac{\mu_0 N_1 N_2 \pi r_1^2}{l} = (4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times (6 \times 10^5) \times (4\pi \times 10^{-4}) = 96\pi^2 \times 10^{-6}\text{ H} \approx \mathbf{0.95\text{ mH}}$.
Numerical 10 Topic 6: AC Generator Peak Voltage
An AC generator armature of $200\text{ turns}$, area $0.06\text{ m}^2$ rotates at $3000\text{ rpm}$ in a uniform magnetic field $B = 0.08\text{ T}$. Calculate the peak generated emf $\varepsilon_0$ in Volts. (Use $\pi = 3.1416$).
$\omega = 2\pi \times (3000/60) = 100\pi\text{ rad/s}$.
$\varepsilon_0 = N B A \omega = 200 \times 0.08 \times 0.06 \times (100\pi) = 96\pi\text{ V} \approx \mathbf{301.6\text{ V}}$.
