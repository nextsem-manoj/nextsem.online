Exercise 6.2 — Q1 Magnetic Flux & Faraday's Law CBSE Board • 3 Marks
Problem Statement

A square loop of side 10 cm and resistance 0.5 Ω is placed vertically in the east-west plane. A uniform magnetic field of 0.10 T is set up across the plane in the north-east direction. The magnetic field is decreased to zero in 0.70 s at a steady rate. Determine the magnitude of induced electromotive force (emf) and current in the loop during this time-interval.

explore Orientation: Normal points East, Field at θ = 45°
Given Parameters
Side a: 10 cm = 0.1 m
R: 0.5 Ω
Binit: 0.10 T
θ: 45°
Δt: 0.70 s
functions Key Governing Formulae
Standard Model
1. Magnetic Flux: ΦB = B · A = B A cos(θ)
2. Faraday's EMF: |ε| = |dΦB / dt| = ΔΦ / Δt
3. Ohm's Law (Induced Current): I = ε / R
account_tree

Step-by-Step Solution

3 Steps
1 Area & Initial Magnetic Flux
Phase 1

First calculate the surface area enclosed by the square wire loop:

A = a² = (0.1 m)² 1.0 × 10−² m²
Since the normal to the vertical east-west plane points East, and the magnetic field is directed North-East, the acute angle between B and normal vector θ = 45°.
Φinitial = B · A · cos(45°) 0.707 × 10−³ Wb
2 Rate of Change of Flux (Induced EMF)
Phase 2

The field steadily drops to zero, yielding final flux Φfinal = 0 Wb over Δt = 0.70 s:

ΔΦ = |Φfinal − Φinit| 0.707 × 10−³ Wb
|ε| = ΔΦ / Δt = (0.707 × 10−³) / 0.70 1.01 × 10−³ V ≈ 1.0 mV
3 Induced Current in Loop
Phase 3

Applying Ohm's Law using the loop's internal resistance R = 0.5 Ω:

I = |ε| / R = (1.01 × 10−³ V) / (0.5 Ω) 2.02 × 10−³ A ≈ 2.0 mA
verified Verified Final Answer
NCERT Benchmark
Magnitude of Induced EMF: |ε| = 1.0 mV
Magnitude of Induced Current: I = 2.0 mA
navigation Direction: East to West along the bottom edge (Lenz's Law opposition)
lightbulb
Key Conceptual Takeaway Lenz's Law Core Principle
expand_less

By Lenz's law, as the external magnetic flux decreasingly collapses through the coil, the induced magnetic field vehemently opposes this reduction. It generates a clockwise-directed circulation that sustains flux parallel to the original field trajectory.
