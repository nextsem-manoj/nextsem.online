🌟 Everyday Applications & Biological Importance

Alcohols, phenols and ethers are the basic compounds for the formation of detergents, antiseptics and fragrances, respectively:

Alcohols ➔ Detergents & Solvents
Ethanol (C₂H₅OH)

• Ordinary spirit used for polishing wooden furniture is chiefly ethanol (C₂H₅OH).
• Industrial precursors for biodegradable surfactant detergents.

Phenols ➔ Antiseptics & Disinfectants
Carbolic Acid / Phenol (C₆H₅OH)

• Exhibits powerful antimicrobial & germicidal properties.
• Primary active agent in household disinfectants, soaps, and pharmaceuticals.

Ethers ➔ Fragrances & Perfumes
Alkoxy & Aryloxy Derivatives

• Pleasant aromatic qualities and stable non-polar solvents.
• Used extensively in flavorings, perfumes, and anesthetic formulations.

💡 Did You Know? The sugar we eat (sucrose), the cotton used for fabrics (cellulose), and the paper we use for writing are all made up of chemical compounds containing –OH (hydroxyl) groups!
🧪 Fundamental Chemical Definitions & Formulae
Alcohol (R—OH)
Aliphatic Hydroxyl Derivative

Formed when a hydrogen atom in an aliphatic hydrocarbon is replaced by a –OH group.
Examples: Methanol (CH₃OH), Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH).

Phenol (Ar—OH)
Aromatic Hydroxyl Derivative

Formed when a hydrogen atom in an aromatic hydrocarbon is replaced by a –OH group directly attached to a benzene ring.
Example: Phenol (C₆H₅OH).

Ether (R—O—R′ / Ar—O—R)
Alkoxy / Aryloxy Derivative

Formed by substitution of H in a hydrocarbon by an alkoxy (R—O—) or aryloxy (Ar—O—) group, or by substituting –H of –OH in alcohol/phenol by alkyl/aryl.
Example: Dimethyl ether (CH₃OCH₃).

📐 Structural Classification Taxonomy

A. By Number of –OH Groups (Mono, Di, Tri, Polyhydric)

Monohydric

Contains one –OH group.
e.g., Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH), Phenol (C₆H₅OH)

Dihydric (Glycols)

Contains two –OH groups on different carbons.
e.g., Ethane-1,2-diol (CH₂OH—CH₂OH), Catechol

Trihydric (Glycerols)

Contains three –OH groups.
e.g., Propane-1,2,3-triol (CH₂OH—CHOH—CH₂OH)

B. By Carbon Hybridisation Attached to –OH

Alkyl Alcohols [C(sp³)—OH]

• Primary (1°): R—CH₂—OH
• Secondary (2°): R₂CH—OH
• Tertiary (3°): R₃C—OH

Allylic Alcohols [C(sp³)—OH]

–OH attached to sp³ carbon adjacent to C=C double bond.
e.g., Prop-2-en-1-ol (CH₂=CH—CH₂OH)
(Can be 1°, 2°, 3°).

Benzylic Alcohols [C(sp³)—OH]

–OH attached to sp³ carbon adjacent to an aromatic ring.
e.g., Benzyl alcohol (C₆H₅—CH₂OH)
(Can be 1°, 2°, 3°).

Vinylic Alcohols [C(sp²)—OH]

–OH attached directly to sp² carbon of double bond.
e.g., Vinyl alcohol (CH₂=CH—OH).
