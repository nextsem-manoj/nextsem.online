📄 Page 5: Let us Think (Comprehension Questions)

1. What was Gopi doing on the veranda?
Answer: Gopi was sitting in the veranda, reading a book.
2. What sound did Gopi hear before he went to the garden?
Answer: He heard something rustling past and falling with a thud in the garden.
3. Describe Gopi’s search for the ball.
Answer: Gopi searched in the thick grass near the fence, looked among the bushes, and finally discovered the white hockey ball resting on a heap of dry leaves.
5. Who grabbed the ball from Gopi? How did the game start?
Answer: Manoj grabbed the ball and tossed it to Deepak. Manoj went to the goalpost as goalkeeper, Gopi stood at the centre, passed to Deepak, who drove it towards Ramani, and Ramani dribbled it to Jay.
6. Why did everyone laugh at the end of the story?
Answer: Jay hit the ball so hard that it flew past the gate and landed directly into the basket of a passing scooter! The scooterist drove away unaware, and the ball was gone with the scooter!

📖 Page 7: Complete Story with Conjunctions

Gopi completed his work because he wanted to go outside and play. However when he reached the playground nobody was there. Since his friends were yet to join him, he decided to take a walk. After 15 minutes, five of them came and everyone started to discuss the games. They decided to play hopscotch when they got to know that Ramani would not be joining them.

🌟 Page 16: Major Dhyan Chand Mini-Biography

Major Dhyan Chand — "The Wizard of Hockey"

• Sport: Field Hockey (Legendary Center Forward)

• Born: 29 August 1905 in Prayagraj (Allahabad), Uttar Pradesh

• Glorious Achievements: Led India to three consecutive Olympic Gold Medals in 1928 (Amsterdam), 1932 (Los Angeles), and 1936 (Berlin). He scored over 400 international goals.

• Why Inspiring: Regarded as the greatest hockey player in world history. His magical ball dribbling amazed fans worldwide. His birthday (29 August) is proudly celebrated as India's National Sports Day, and India's highest sporting honour, the Major Dhyan Chand Khel Ratna Award, bears his name.
