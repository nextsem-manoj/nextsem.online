The figure below shows a circle with centre $O$ having two parallel chords $AB = 24\text{ cm}$ and $CD = 10\text{ cm}$ located on the same side of the centre. The perpendicular from $O$ intersects $AB$ at $M$ and $CD$ at $N$. If the distance between the chords is $MN = 7\text{ cm}$, find the radius $r$ of the circle.

Figure 5.16: Parallel chords AB and CD on same side with distance MN = 7 cm
A 13 cm
B 15 cm
C 12 cm
D 17 cm
Self-Evaluation:
🎯 CBSE / NEP Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Step 1
By Theorem 5, the perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord: AM = 12 cm, CN = 5 cm. Let distance OM = d cm. Then ON = (d + 7) cm.
½ Mark
Step 2
By Baudhāyana-Pythagoras theorem: In △OMA, r² = d² + 12² = d² + 144. In △ONC, r² = (d + 7)² + 5² = d² + 14d + 49 + 25 = d² + 14d + 74. Equating both gives d² + 144 = d² + 14d + 74 ⇒ 14d = 70 ⇒ d = 5 cm. Therefore, r = √(5² + 12²) = √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13 cm.
½ Mark
Correct Answer: Option A — 13 cm

Q11 🔢 Numerical
Ch 4 Medium-Hard 1 Mark
📖 Source: Ganita Manjari Part 1 §4.1 (Example 1 & Think and Reflect, Page 69)

Example 1 in Ganita Manjari discovers that for any three consecutive squares $(n-1)^2, n^2, (n+1)^2$, the quantity $P(n) = (n-1)^2 + (n+1)^2 - 2n^2$ is constant. Furthermore, for four consecutive squares $(n-1)^2, n^2, (n+1)^2, (n+2)^2$, let $Q(n) = (n-1)^2 + (n+2)^2 - n^2 - (n+1)^2$. Evaluate the exact numerical values of $P(999)$ and $Q(999)$.

A P(999) = 2 and Q(999) = 4
B P(999) = 0 and Q(999) = 2
C P(999) = 2 and Q(999) = 6
D P(999) = 1998 and Q(999) = 3996
Self-Evaluation:
🎯 CBSE / NEP Step-by-Step Marking Scheme
Step 1
Expand P(n): (n - 1)² + (n + 1)² - 2n² = (n² - 2n + 1) + (n² + 2n + 1) - 2n² = 2n² + 2 - 2n² = 2. This is an algebraic identity independent of n, so P(999) = 2.
½ Mark
Step 2
Expand Q(n): (n - 1)² + (n + 2)² - [n² + (n + 1)²] = (n² - 2n + 1 + n² + 4n + 4) - (n² + n² + 2n + 1) = (2n² + 2n + 5) - (2n² + 2n + 1) = 4. Hence Q(999) = 4.
½ Mark
Correct Answer: Option A — P(999) = 2 and Q(999) = 4
💡 NEP Competency: Algebraic Proof of Numerical Invariants & Induction Patterns.
