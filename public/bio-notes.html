1. Introduction: The Ubiquitous Microbes

Microorganisms are the major microscopic components of biological systems on Earth. While they are often viewed solely as agents of disease, the vast majority are vital contributors to human survival, nutrition, medicine, and ecosystem balance.

Omnipresence: Microbes thrive everywhere—in soil, water, air, inside bodies of animals and plants, and in extreme environments where no other organisms can survive, such as thermal vents/geysers ($>100^\circ\text{C}$), under meters-thick layers of snow, deep in the soil, and in highly acidic settings.

Microbes include Protozoa, Bacteria, Fungi, microscopic plant and animal Viruses, Viroids, and Prions (proteinaceous infectious agents). In laboratory settings, bacteria and fungi are cultivated on nutritive media into macroscopically visible colonies.

2. Microbes in Household Products

A. Curd Production from Milk

  • Organism: Lactobacillus and other Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB).
  • Mechanism: A starter/inoculum containing millions of LAB is inoculated into milk at an ambient temperature. LAB multiply and produce lactic acid, which coagulates and partially digests milk proteins (casein).
  • Nutritional & Health Boost: Converts milk into curd and significantly enhances its nutritional value by increasing Vitamin B₁₂. In the human digestive tract, LAB also checks the proliferation of disease-causing pathogens.

B. Dough for Dosa, Idli, and Bread

  • Dosa and Idli: Batter is fermented naturally by bacteria. The puffed-up appearance of the batter is caused by bubbles of CO₂ gas released during anaerobic respiration.
  • Bread: Fermented by Baker’s Yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae). CO₂ released during fermentation makes the bread soft, porous, and spongy.

C. Traditional Foods & Cheese

  • Toddy: A traditional South Indian beverage prepared by fermenting sap tapped from palm trees.
  • Swiss Cheese: Characterized by large circular holes formed by large volumes of CO₂ released by the bacterium Propionibacterium sharmanii.
  • Roquefort Cheese: Ripened by a specific fungus (Penicillium roqueforti), imparting its distinctive texture and flavor.

3. Microbes in Industrial Products

Large-scale commercial production takes place inside massive steel vessels called Fermentors.

A. Fermented Beverages

Brewer’s Yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae) ferments malted cereals and fruit sugars to synthesize ethanol:

  • Without Distillation: Wine and Beer (moderate alcohol percentage).
  • With Distillation: Whisky, Brandy, and Rum (concentrated alcohol).

B. Antibiotics

  • Discovery: Alexander Fleming (1928) observed that the mould Penicillium notatum inhibited the growth of Staphylococci bacteria on an unwashed Petri plate.
  • Clinical Potency: Established by Ernest Chain and Howard Florey during World War II. Fleming, Chain, and Florey were jointly awarded the Nobel Prize in 1945.
  • Antibiotics treat deadly bacterial diseases such as plague, whooping cough (kali khansi), diphtheria (gal ghotu), and leprosy (kusht rog).

C. Chemicals, Enzymes & Bioactive Molecules

Key Bioactive Molecules to Memorize:
  • Streptokinase (from Streptococcus): Modified by genetic engineering; acts as a "Clot Buster" to dissolve intravascular clots in patients who have suffered myocardial infarction (heart attack).
  • Cyclosporin A (from fungus Trichoderma polysporum): An essential immunosuppressive drug administered during organ-transplant surgeries to prevent graft rejection.
  • Statins (from yeast Monascus purpureus): A blood-cholesterol lowering agent; functions via competitive inhibition of the rate-limiting enzyme in cholesterol synthesis.
  • Lipases: Formulated in laundry detergents to dissolve oily and lipid stains.
  • Pectinases & Proteases: Used to clarify commercially bottled fruit juices.

4. Microbes in Sewage Treatment

Municipal wastewater (sewage) carries high loads of human excreta, organic waste, and pathogens. It is treated in Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs) in two successive stages:

Stage 1: Primary Treatment (Physical)

  • Sequential Filtration: Removes floating coarse debris.
  • Sedimentation: Heavy grit (sand and small pebbles) settles down by gravity.
  • Settled solids form Primary Sludge, while supernatant liquid is the Primary Effluent.

Stage 2: Secondary Treatment (Biological)

  • Effluent is aerated in large Aeration Tanks with continuous mechanical agitation and oxygen pumping.
  • Useful aerobic microbes form Flocs (masses of bacteria associated with fungal filaments to form mesh-like structures).
  • Flocs consume organic impurities, drastically reducing BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand).
  • BOD Defined: The amount of oxygen consumed if all organic matter in 1 liter of water were oxidized by bacteria. High BOD indicates severe pollution.
  • Once BOD is minimized, effluent passes to a settling tank where flocs settle as Activated Sludge.
  • A small portion of activated sludge is recycled back to the aeration tank as inoculum; the remainder enters Anaerobic Sludge Digesters.
  • Anaerobic bacteria digest the flocs, producing Biogas (Methane, CO₂, and H₂S).

5. Microbes in Biogas Production

Biogas is a combustible fuel composed primarily of Methane (CH₄), CO₂, and H₂. It is produced by specialized anaerobic bacteria known as methanogens (e.g., Methanobacterium).

  • Natural Habitat: Methanogens thrive in anaerobic sludge digesters and in the rumen (first stomach compartment) of ruminant cattle, breaking down cellulose.
  • Biogas Plant Design:
    • Built as a 10 to 15 feet deep concrete digestion well.
    • Fed with cattle dung (gobar) slurry and organic farm refuse.
    • Covered by a floating gas-holder that ascends as gas pressure builds.
    • Spent slurry is discharged through an outlet and used as high-grade organic fertilizer.
    • Popularized in rural India by IARI and KVIC.

6. Microbes as Biocontrol Agents

Biocontrol uses natural biological predators and microbes to regulate insect pests and plant diseases, preventing the environmental toxicity associated with synthetic pesticides:

  • Ladybird Beetle: Controls aphids.
  • Dragonflies: Eradicate mosquitoes.
  • Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt): Dried spores sprayed on crops are consumed by butterfly caterpillars; alkaline gut fluid activates the bacterial crystal endotoxin, killing the pest. Bt-toxin genes have also been introduced into crops (e.g., Bt-cotton).
  • Trichoderma (Fungus): Free-living in plant root ecosystems; suppresses root pathogens.
  • Baculoviruses (Genus Nucleopolyhedrovirus): Pathogens attacking insects and arthropods. They have species-specific, narrow-spectrum activity with zero harmful effects on plants, mammals, birds, fish, or non-target predatory insects, making them premier tools for Integrated Pest Management (IPM).

7. Microbes as Biofertilisers

Biofertilisers are living organisms that enrich the nutrient content of soil sustainably:

  1. Bacteria:
    • Symbiotic: Rhizobium forms nodules on leguminous roots and fixes atmospheric nitrogen into absorbable organic forms.
    • Free-living: Azotobacter and Azospirillum enrich soil nitrogen content independently in arable soils.
  2. Fungi (Mycorrhiza):
    • Symbiotic association between fungi of the genus Glomus and plant roots.
    • Hyphae absorb Phosphorus from the soil and transfer it to the plant.
    • Bestows resistance to root-borne diseases, drought, and salinity.
  3. Cyanobacteria (Blue-Green Algae):
    • Photosynthetic autotrophic nitrogen fixers: Anabaena, Nostoc, Oscillatoria.
    • Serve as indispensable biofertilisers in waterlogged paddy (rice) fields.
