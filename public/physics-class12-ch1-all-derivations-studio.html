Part A: Coulomb's Law & Electric Field (Sections 1.5 – 1.7)

Derivation 1 • NCERT Sec 1.5

Coulomb's Law in Vector Form & Principle of Superposition

📖 Textbook Pages 10–13 • Target: \(\vec{F}_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}\hat{r}_{12}\)
CBSE 3 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(\vec{F}_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q_1 q_2}{r_{12}^2} \hat{r}_{12} = -\vec{F}_{21}\)
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    Let charges \(q_1\) and \(q_2\) be located at position vectors \(\vec{r}_1\) and \(\vec{r}_2\) relative to origin \(O\). The displacement vector from \(q_1\) to \(q_2\) is \(\vec{r}_{21} = \vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1\), with unit vector \(\hat{r}_{21} = \frac{\vec{r}_{21}}{r_{21}}\).
  2. 2
    The electrostatic force \(\vec{F}_{21}\) exerted on \(q_2\) by \(q_1\) is directed along the line joining them:
    \(\vec{F}_{21} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{r_{21}^2} \hat{r}_{21} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{|\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1|^3} (\vec{r}_2 - \vec{r}_1)\)
  3. 3
    Similarly, the force exerted on \(q_1\) by \(q_2\) is:
    \(\vec{F}_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{r_{12}^2} \hat{r}_{12} = -\vec{F}_{21} \quad (\text{since } \hat{r}_{12} = -\hat{r}_{21})\)
    This proves that electrostatic forces obey Newton's Third Law of motion.
💡 Superposition Principle for N Point Charges

The total force on charge \(q_1\) due to \(N-1\) other stationary charges is the vector sum of individual Coulomb forces: \[\vec{F}_1 = \vec{F}_{12} + \vec{F}_{13} + \dots + \vec{F}_{1N} = \frac{q_1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \sum_{i=2}^N \frac{q_i}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_i|^3} (\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_i)\]

Derivation 2 • NCERT Sec 1.7

Electric Field due to an Isolated Point Charge

📖 Textbook Pages 15–18 • Target: \(\vec{E} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{q}{r^2}\hat{r}\)
CBSE 2 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q}{r^2} \hat{r}\)
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    Place an infinitesimal positive test charge \(q_0\) at distance \(r\) from source charge \(q\) at origin \(O\).
  2. 2
    By Coulomb's law, the force on test charge \(q_0\) is:
    \(\vec{F} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q q_0}{r^2} \hat{r}\)
  3. 3
    Electric field \(\vec{E}\) is defined as the force per unit test charge in the limit \(q_0 \to 0\):
    \(\vec{E} = \lim_{q_0 \to 0} \frac{\vec{F}}{q_0} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q}{r^2} \hat{r}\)

Part B: Field of an Electric Dipole & Torque (Sections 1.10 – 1.11)

Derivation 3 • NCERT Sec 1.10.1 (a)

Electric Field of Dipole on the Axial Line (End-On Position)

📖 Textbook Pages 22–24 • Target: \(\vec{E}_{\text{axial}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{2\vec{p}}{r^3}\)
CBSE 5 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(\vec{E}_{\text{axial}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2\vec{p} r}{(r^2 - a^2)^2} \xrightarrow{r \gg a} \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2\vec{p}}{r^3}\)
📐 Diagram: Axial Line Field
Figure 1.1: Axial electric field calculation at point \(P\) distance \(r\) from midpoint \(O\). Since \(P\) is closer to \(+q\), \(E_+ > E_-\).
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    Consider an electric dipole consisting of charges \(-q\) and \(+q\) separated by distance \(2a\). Point \(P\) lies on the dipole axis at distance \(r\) from center \(O\).
    • Distance from \(+q\) to \(P = r - a\)
    • Distance from \(-q\) to \(P = r + a\)
  2. 2
    Electric field due to \(+q\) (directed away from dipole):
    \(\vec{E}_+ = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q}{(r - a)^2} \hat{p}\)
    Electric field due to \(-q\) (directed toward dipole):
    \(\vec{E}_- = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q}{(r + a)^2} \hat{p}\)
  3. 3
    Net electric field \(\vec{E}_{\text{axial}} = \vec{E}_+ + \vec{E}_-\):
    \(\vec{E}_{\text{axial}} = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left[ \frac{1}{(r - a)^2} - \frac{1}{(r + a)^2} \right] \hat{p} = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \left[ \frac{(r + a)^2 - (r - a)^2}{(r^2 - a^2)^2} \right] \hat{p}\)
    Since \((r+a)^2 - (r-a)^2 = 4ar\):
    \(\vec{E}_{\text{axial}} = \frac{q (4ar)}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 (r^2 - a^2)^2} \hat{p} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2(2qa) r}{(r^2 - a^2)^2} \hat{p}\)
  4. 4
    Substitute electric dipole moment \(\vec{p} = 2qa \hat{p}\):
    \(\vec{E}_{\text{axial}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2\vec{p} r}{(r^2 - a^2)^2}\)
    For a short dipole (\(r \gg a\)), neglect \(a^2\) in denominator:
    \(\vec{E}_{\text{axial}} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2\vec{p}}{r^3}\)
💡 Direction Insight

On the axial line, the net electric field \(\vec{E}_{\text{axial}}\) is always in the same direction as the electric dipole moment \(\vec{p}\).

Derivation 4 • NCERT Sec 1.10.1 (b)

Electric Field of Dipole on the Equatorial Line (Broadside-On Position)

📖 Textbook Pages 24–25 • Target: \(\vec{E}_{\text{eq}} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{\vec{p}}{r^3}\)
CBSE 5 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(\vec{E}_{\text{eq}} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{\vec{p}}{(r^2 + a^2)^{3/2}} \xrightarrow{r \gg a} -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{\vec{p}}{r^3}\)
📐 Diagram: Equatorial Cancellation
Figure 1.2: Vertical sine components cancel by symmetry (\(E_+\sin\theta = E_-\sin\theta\)); horizontal cosine components add together in direction opposite to \(\vec{p}\).
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    Point \(P\) lies on the equatorial bisector at distance \(r\) from center \(O\). Distance from each charge to \(P\) is identical by Pythagoras theorem:
    \(d = \sqrt{r^2 + a^2}\)
  2. 2
    Magnitudes of electric fields due to \(+q\) and \(-q\) are equal:
    \(E_+ = E_- = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q}{r^2 + a^2}\)
  3. 3
    Resolving Components:
    • The components perpendicular to dipole axis (\(E_+\sin\theta\) and \(E_-\sin\theta\)) are equal and opposite, so they cancel out.
    • The components parallel to dipole axis add up in direction opposite to \(\hat{p}\):
    \(E_{\text{net}} = E_+\cos\theta + E_-\cos\theta = 2 E_+ \cos\theta\)
  4. 4
    From geometry, \(\cos\theta = \frac{a}{\sqrt{r^2 + a^2}}\):
    \(E_{\text{net}} = 2 \left( \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q}{r^2 + a^2} \right) \left( \frac{a}{\sqrt{r^2 + a^2}} \right) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2qa}{(r^2 + a^2)^{3/2}}\)
    Since \(\vec{p} = 2qa \hat{p}\) and the net field points opposite to \(\hat{p}\):
    \(\vec{E}_{\text{eq}} = -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{\vec{p}}{(r^2 + a^2)^{3/2}} \xrightarrow{r \gg a} -\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{\vec{p}}{r^3}\)
💡 The 2:1 Golden Ratio

For a short dipole at equal distances \(r\): \[E_{\text{axial}} = 2 \cdot E_{\text{eq}}\] Axial field is twice the equatorial field, and points in opposite directions relative to \(\vec{p}\).

Derivation 5 • NCERT Sec 1.10.1 Ext

Electric Field of Dipole at Any General Point \((r, \theta)\)

📖 General Formulation • Target: \(E = \frac{p}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^3}\sqrt{3\cos^2\theta + 1}\)
CBSE 5 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(E = \frac{p}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^3}\sqrt{3\cos^2\theta + 1} \quad \text{and} \quad \tan\alpha = \frac{1}{2}\tan\theta\)
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    Resolve dipole moment vector \(\vec{p}\) into two mutually perpendicular components:
    • \(p\cos\theta\) along radial direction \(\vec{r}\) (point \(P\) is on axial line of this component).
    • \(p\sin\theta\) perpendicular to \(\vec{r}\) (point \(P\) is on equatorial line of this component).
  2. 2
    Using the axial and equatorial results for short dipole:
    \(E_r = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{2p\cos\theta}{r^3} \quad \text{and} \quad E_\theta = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{p\sin\theta}{r^3}\)
  3. 3
    The two components are mutually perpendicular. The resultant electric field magnitude:
    \(E = \sqrt{E_r^2 + E_\theta^2} = \frac{p}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^3} \sqrt{(2\cos\theta)^2 + (\sin\theta)^2} = \frac{p}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^3}\sqrt{4\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta}\)
    Substitute \(\sin^2\theta = 1 - \cos^2\theta\):
    \(E = \frac{p}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^3} \sqrt{3\cos^2\theta + 1}\)
Derivation 6 • NCERT Sec 1.11

Torque on an Electric Dipole in a Uniform External Field

📖 Textbook Pages 25–27 • Target: \(\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}\)
CBSE 3 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E} \implies \tau = p E \sin\theta\)
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    In a uniform field \(\vec{E}\), force on \(+q\) is \(\vec{F}_+ = +q\vec{E}\) and on \(-q\) is \(\vec{F}_- = -q\vec{E}\).
    Net Translational Force \(\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = +q\vec{E} - q\vec{E} = 0\).
  2. 2
    Since the lines of action of the two equal and opposite forces do not coincide, they constitute a couple.
    Perpendicular distance between lines of action = \(2a\sin\theta\).
  3. 3
    Magnitude of torque = \(\text{Force} \times \text{Perpendicular arm}\):
    \(\tau = q E (2a\sin\theta) = (2qa) E \sin\theta = p E \sin\theta\)
    In vector notation:
    \(\vec{\tau} = \vec{p} \times \vec{E}\)

Part C: Electric Flux & Gauss's Law (Section 1.13)

Derivation 7 • NCERT Sec 1.13

Gauss's Law Proof for a Point Charge inside a Closed Surface

📖 Textbook Pages 29–33 • Target: \(\Phi = \oint \vec{E}\cdot d\vec{A} = \frac{q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}\)
CBSE 3 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(\Phi_E = \oint_S \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = \frac{q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0}\)
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    Consider a single point charge \(q\) situated at the center of a spherical Gaussian surface of radius \(r\).
  2. 2
    By symmetry, the electric field \(\vec{E}\) has the same magnitude at every point on the sphere and points radially outward:
    \(\vec{E} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q}{r^2} \hat{r}\)
  3. 3
    Area vector \(d\vec{A} = dA \hat{r}\). Since \(\vec{E} \parallel d\vec{A}\), \(\vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = E dA\):
    \(\Phi = \oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = \oint E dA = E \oint dA = \left( \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q}{r^2} \right) (4\pi r^2) = \frac{q}{\varepsilon_0}\)

Part D: Applications of Gauss's Law (Section 1.14)

Derivation 8 • NCERT Sec 1.14.1

Field due to an Infinitely Long Straight Uniformly Charged Wire

📖 Textbook Pages 34–36 • Target: \(E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}\)
CBSE 5 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(\vec{E} = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r} \hat{n}\)
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    Let an infinitely long straight wire have uniform linear charge density \(\lambda\). By cylindrical symmetry, electric field \(\vec{E}\) is directed radially outward.
  2. 2
    Construct Gaussian Surface: Choose a coaxial cylinder of radius \(r\) and length \(l\).
    • Flux through flat end caps: \(\Phi_{\text{ends}} = 0\) (since \(\vec{E} \perp d\vec{A}\)).
    • Flux through curved surface: \(\vec{E} \parallel d\vec{A}\), magnitude \(E\) is constant.
    \(\Phi_{\text{curved}} = E (2\pi r l)\)
  3. 3
    Total charge enclosed inside the cylinder: \(q_{\text{enc}} = \lambda l\).
  4. 4
    Apply Gauss's Law:
    \(\Phi = \frac{q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E (2\pi r l) = \frac{\lambda l}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = \frac{\lambda}{2\pi\varepsilon_0 r}\)
💡 Graph: Distance Dependence

For line charge: \(E \propto \frac{1}{r}\) (falls slower than point charge \(1/r^2\)).

Derivation 9 • NCERT Sec 1.14.2

Field due to a Uniformly Charged Infinite Plane Sheet

📖 Textbook Pages 36–37 • Target: \(E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\)
CBSE 3/5 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(\vec{E} = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} \hat{n}\)
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    Consider an infinite thin plane sheet with uniform surface charge density \(\sigma\). Field \(\vec{E}\) is directed normal to the plane on both sides.
  2. 2
    Construct Gaussian Surface: Choose a cylinder (pillbox) of cross-sectional area \(A\) penetrating through the sheet.
    • Flux through curved side = \(0\) (since \(\vec{E} \perp d\vec{A}\)).
    • Flux through both flat end caps = \(E A + E A = 2 E A\).
  3. 3
    Total charge enclosed inside pillbox: \(q_{\text{enc}} = \sigma A\).
  4. 4
    By Gauss's Law:
    \(2 E A = \frac{\sigma A}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\)
💡 Distance Independence

Notice that \(E\) is completely independent of distance \(r\) from the sheet! The field remains uniform throughout space.

Derivation 10 • NCERT Sec 1.14.2 Ext

Field between Two Oppositely Charged Parallel Infinite Sheets

📖 Textbook Pages 37–38 • Target: \(E = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}\)
CBSE 3 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(E_{\text{between}} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}, \quad E_{\text{outside}} = 0\)
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    Two parallel infinite plane sheets with surface densities \(+\sigma\) and \(-\sigma\).
  2. 2
    In Region I (left of \(+\sigma\)): \(E_I = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} - \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = 0\).
  3. 3
    In Region III (right of \(-\sigma\)): \(E_{III} = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} - \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = 0\).
  4. 4
    In Region II (between sheets, both fields point from \(+\) to \(-\)):
    \(E_{II} = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} + \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} = \frac{\sigma}{\varepsilon_0}\)
Derivation 11 • NCERT Sec 1.14.3

Field due to a Uniformly Charged Thin Spherical Shell

📖 Textbook Pages 38–40 • Target: \(E = \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2} \text{ (outside)}, E = 0 \text{ (inside)}\)
CBSE 5 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(E(r) = \begin{cases} \frac{q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 r^2} = \frac{\sigma R^2}{\varepsilon_0 r^2} & (r > R) \\ 0 & (r < R) \end{cases}\)
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    Case 1: Field Outside the Shell (\(r > R\)): Construct a concentric spherical Gaussian surface of radius \(r > R\).
    \(\Phi = \oint \vec{E} \cdot d\vec{A} = E (4\pi r^2)\)
    By Gauss's Law:
    \(E (4\pi r^2) = \frac{q}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q}{r^2}\)
  2. 2
    Case 2: Field Inside the Shell (\(r < R\)): Construct a concentric spherical Gaussian surface of radius \(r < R\). Since all charge resides on the outer surface, the enclosed charge is zero: \(q_{\text{enc}} = 0\).
    \(E (4\pi r^2) = \frac{0}{\varepsilon_0} \implies E = 0\)

Part E: Charged Sphere & Circular Ring (Extended Competency)

Derivation 12 • Solid Non-Conducting Sphere

Field due to a Uniformly Charged Solid Sphere (Volume Density \(\rho\))

📖 Extended Syllabus • Target: \(E_{\text{in}} = \frac{\rho r}{3\varepsilon_0}\)
CBSE 5 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(E_{\text{inside}} = \frac{q r}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R^3} = \frac{\rho r}{3\varepsilon_0} \quad (E \propto r)\)
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    For an insulating solid sphere with uniform volume charge density \(\rho = \frac{q}{\frac{4}{3}\pi R^3}\), inside Gaussian sphere of radius \(r < R\) encloses charge:
    \(q_{\text{enc}} = \rho \left( \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \right) = q \left( \frac{r^3}{R^3} \right)\)
  2. 2
    Applying Gauss's Law:
    \(E (4\pi r^2) = \frac{q_{\text{enc}}}{\varepsilon_0} = \frac{q r^3}{\varepsilon_0 R^3} \implies E(r) = \frac{q r}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 R^3} = \frac{\rho r}{3\varepsilon_0}\)
Derivation 13 • Circular Ring Axis

Electric Field on the Axis of a Uniformly Charged Circular Ring

📖 Continuous Charge Application • Target: \(E = \frac{q x}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 (x^2+R^2)^{3/2}}\)
CBSE 5 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(E(x) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q x}{(x^2 + R^2)^{3/2}}\)
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    Consider a ring of radius \(R\) with total charge \(q\). Point \(P\) lies on its axis at distance \(x\) from center \(O\). Distance from any ring charge element \(dq\) to \(P\) is \(r = \sqrt{x^2 + R^2}\).
  2. 2
    By symmetry, perpendicular components \(dE\sin\theta\) of diametrically opposite elements cancel out. Only axial components \(dE\cos\theta\) survive:
    \(dE_x = dE \cos\theta = \left( \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{dq}{x^2 + R^2} \right) \left( \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2 + R^2}} \right) = \frac{x dq}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 (x^2 + R^2)^{3/2}}\)
  3. 3
    Integrating over the entire ring (\(\int dq = q\)):
    \(E(x) = \frac{q x}{4\pi\varepsilon_0 (x^2 + R^2)^{3/2}}\)
💡 Maximum Field Position

Setting \(\frac{dE}{dx} = 0\) gives the point of maximum electric field at: \[x = \pm \frac{R}{\sqrt{2}}\]

Derivation 14 • Dipole Rotation

Work Done in Rotating an Electric Dipole in Uniform Electric Field

📖 Textbook Pages 26–27 • Target: \(W = pE(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)\)
CBSE 3 Marks ▼
Core Master Formula:
\(W = pE(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)\)
📝 Step-by-Step Proof
  1. 1
    Restoring torque on dipole at angle \(\theta\): \(\tau = pE\sin\theta\).
  2. 2
    Work done in rotating dipole through \(d\theta\):
    \(dW = \tau d\theta = pE\sin\theta d\theta\)
  3. 3
    Total work done from \(\theta_1\) to \(\theta_2\):
    \(W = \int_{\theta_1}^{\theta_2} pE\sin\theta d\theta = pE [-\cos\theta]_{\theta_1}^{\theta_2} = pE(\cos\theta_1 - \cos\theta_2)\)
