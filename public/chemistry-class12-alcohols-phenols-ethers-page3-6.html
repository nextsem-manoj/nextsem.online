🌟 Ethers Classification & Vinylic Alcohols (Page 3 / Text Page 195)
Symmetrical / Simple Ethers
Identical Groups (R—O—R)

Both alkyl or aryl groups attached to oxygen are the same.
• Diethyl ether: C₂H₅—O—C₂H₅ (Ethoxyethane)
• Dimethyl ether: CH₃—O—CH₃ (Methoxymethane)

Unsymmetrical / Mixed Ethers
Different Groups (R—O—R′ / Ar—O—R)

The two alkyl/aryl groups attached to oxygen are different.
• Ethyl methyl ether: C₂H₅—O—CH₃
• Anisole (Methyl phenyl ether): C₆H₅—O—CH₃
• Phenetole (Ethyl phenyl ether): C₆H₅—O—C₂H₅

Vinylic Alcohols [C(sp²)—OH]
Direct Double Bond Attachment

–OH group is bonded directly to an sp²-hybridised vinylic carbon of a C=C double bond.
• Vinyl alcohol: CH₂=CH—OH (Ethenol)

🔬 3D Structure & Bond Geometry Comparison (Pages 6 & 7 / Text Pages 198–199)
Methanol (Alcohol)
CH₃—OH

• C—O—H Angle: 108.9° (< 109.5°)
• C—O Length: 142 pm
• O—H Length: 96 pm
• 💡 Reason: Repulsion between the two unshared lone electron pairs on Oxygen compresses the angle.

Phenol
C₆H₅—OH

• C—O—H Angle: 109°
• C—O Length: 136 pm (Shorter!)
• O—H Length: 96 pm
• 💡 Reason: (1) Partial double bond character via resonance with ring; (2) sp² carbon has higher s-character.

Methoxymethane (Ether)
CH₃—O—CH₃

• C—O—C Angle: 111.7° (> 109.5°)
• C—O Length: 141 pm
• 💡 Reason: Strong steric repulsion between the two bulky methyl (–CH₃) groups widens the angle!

⭐ Exam-Winning Insight: Why is the C—O bond in Phenol (136 pm) significantly shorter than in Methanol (142 pm)?
1. Conjugation: The unshared electron pair on oxygen conjugates with the benzene ring $\pi$-system, creating partial double bond character.
2. Hybridisation: In phenol, oxygen is bonded to an sp² carbon (33.3% s-character), which is more electronegative than the sp³ carbon (25% s-character) in methanol.
