Topic 1: Magnetic Flux ($\Phi_B = \vec{B} \cdot \vec{A}$)

Numerical 1 Flux in 3D Vector Field
A square coil of side $20\text{ cm}$ having $50\text{ turns}$ is placed in the $X\text{-}Y$ plane. A uniform magnetic field $\vec{B} = (0.2\hat{i} + 0.3\hat{j} + 0.4\hat{k})\text{ T}$ is applied. Calculate the total magnetic flux linked with the coil in Webers ($\text{Wb}$).
1. Area vector $\vec{A} = (0.20\text{ m})^2 \hat{k} = 0.04\hat{k}\text{ m}^2$.
2. Flux through 1 turn: $\Phi_1 = \vec{B} \cdot \vec{A} = (0.2\hat{i} + 0.3\hat{j} + 0.4\hat{k}) \cdot 0.04\hat{k} = 0.4 \times 0.04 = 0.016\text{ Wb}$.
3. Total Flux $\Phi_{\text{total}} = N \Phi_1 = 50 \times 0.016 = \mathbf{0.80\text{ Wb}}$.
Numerical 2 Tilted Plane Flux Calculation
A circular loop of radius $14\text{ cm}$ is placed in a uniform magnetic field of $0.5\text{ T}$ such that the normal to the loop makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with the field lines. Find the magnetic flux through the loop in milliwebers ($\text{mWb}$). (Take $\pi = 22/7$).
1. Area $A = \pi r^2 = \frac{22}{7} \times (0.14)^2 = \frac{22}{7} \times 0.0196 = 0.0616\text{ m}^2$.
2. $\Phi = B A \cos\theta = 0.5 \times 0.0616 \times \cos(60^\circ) = 0.0308 \times 0.5 = 0.0154\text{ Wb}$.
3. In milliwebers: $0.0154 \times 10^3 = \mathbf{15.4\text{ mWb}}$.

Topic 2: Faraday's Law & Total Induced Charge

Numerical 3 Flipping Coil Induced Charge
A coil of $200\text{ turns}$, mean area $4\text{ cm}^2$, and resistance $5\,\Omega$ is placed normal to a magnetic field $B$. When the coil is quickly flipped by $180^\circ$, a charge of $3.2 \times 10^{-4}\text{ C}$ flows through it. Calculate the magnetic field $B$ in Teslas ($\text{T}$).
1. Change in flux: $\Delta\Phi = 2BA$.
2. Total charge: $q = \frac{N \Delta\Phi}{R} = \frac{2 N B A}{R} \implies B = \frac{q R}{2 N A}$.
3. $B = \frac{(3.2 \times 10^{-4}\text{ C}) \times 5\,\Omega}{2 \times 200 \times (4 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2)} = \frac{1.6 \times 10^{-3}}{0.16} = \mathbf{0.01\text{ T}}$.
Numerical 4 Time-Varying Current inside Solenoid
A long solenoid has $15\text{ turns/cm}$. A small search loop of area $2.0\text{ cm}^2$ is placed inside it normal to its axis. If current in the solenoid changes steadily from $2.0\text{ A}$ to $4.0\text{ A}$ in $0.1\text{ s}$, calculate the magnitude of the induced emf in microvolts ($\mu\text{V}$). (Use $\pi = 3.1416$).
1. Turns per meter $n = 15\text{ turns/cm} = 1500\text{ turns/m}$.
2. $\frac{dI}{dt} = \frac{4.0 - 2.0}{0.1} = 20\text{ A/s}$.
3. $\frac{dB}{dt} = \mu_0 n \frac{dI}{dt} = (4\pi \times 10^{-7}) \times 1500 \times 20 = 0.0377\text{ T/s}$.
4. $\varepsilon = A \frac{dB}{dt} = (2.0 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2) \times 0.0377 = 7.54 \times 10^{-6}\text{ V} = \mathbf{7.54\,\mu\text{V}}$.

Topic 3: Motional EMF & Mechanical Power Balance

Numerical 5 Falling Horizontal Wire in Earth's Field
A horizontal straight wire $10\text{ m}$ long extending from east to west is falling vertically at a speed of $5.0\text{ m/s}$ at right angles to the horizontal component of Earth's magnetic field $B_H = 0.30 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}$. Calculate the instantaneous induced emf in millivolts ($\text{mV}$).
1. $\varepsilon = B_H \times l \times v = (0.30 \times 10^{-4}\text{ T}) \times 10\text{ m} \times 5.0\text{ m/s}$.
2. $\varepsilon = 1.5 \times 10^{-3}\text{ V} = \mathbf{1.5\text{ mV}}$.
Numerical 6 Sliding Rail Mechanical Power Balance
A conducting rod of length $l = 0.4\text{ m}$ slides on frictionless rails at constant speed $v = 6\text{ m/s}$ in a vertical field $B = 0.75\text{ T}$. The total resistance is $R = 2.0\,\Omega$. Calculate the mechanical power required to maintain this motion in Watts ($\text{W}$).
1. Induced EMF: $\varepsilon = Blv = 0.75 \times 0.4 \times 6 = 1.8\text{ V}$.
2. Induced Current: $I = \varepsilon / R = 1.8 / 2 = 0.9\text{ A}$.
3. Mechanical Power: $P = I^2 R = (0.9)^2 \times 2 = 0.81 \times 2 = \mathbf{1.62\text{ W}}$.

Topic 4: Rotational Motional EMF

Numerical 7 Rotating Metallic Rod on Ring
A $1.0\text{ m}$ long metallic rod is rotated with an angular frequency of $400\text{ rad/s}$ about an axis normal to the rod passing through one end. A uniform magnetic field of $0.5\text{ T}$ parallel to the axis exists everywhere. Calculate the induced emf developed in Volts ($\text{V}$).
1. $\varepsilon = \frac{1}{2} B \omega R^2$.
2. $\varepsilon = \frac{1}{2} \times 0.5\text{ T} \times 400\text{ rad/s} \times (1.0\text{ m})^2 = \mathbf{100\text{ V}}$.
Numerical 8 Rotating Wheel with 10 Spokes
A wheel with $10\text{ metallic spokes}$, each $0.5\text{ m}$ long, is rotated at $120\text{ rpm}$ in a plane normal to a magnetic field of $0.4\text{ Gauss}$ ($1\text{ G} = 10^{-4}\text{ T}$). Calculate the induced emf between the axle and rim in microvolts ($\mu\text{V}$). (Use $\pi = 3.1416$).
1. $\omega = 2\pi \times (120/60) = 4\pi\text{ rad/s}$.
2. $\varepsilon = \frac{1}{2} B \omega R^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times (0.4 \times 10^{-4}) \times (4\pi) \times (0.5)^2 = 2\pi \times 10^{-5}\text{ V} \approx 6.28 \times 10^{-5}\text{ V} = \mathbf{62.8\,\mu\text{V}}$.

Topic 5: Self-Inductance & Energy Density

Numerical 9 Circuit Back EMF Calculation
Current in a circuit falls from $5.0\text{ A}$ to $0.0\text{ A}$ in $0.1\text{ s}$. If an average emf of $200\text{ V}$ is induced, calculate the self-inductance of the circuit in Henrys ($\text{H}$).
1. $|\varepsilon| = L \frac{|\Delta I|}{\Delta t} \implies L = \frac{|\varepsilon|}{\Delta I / \Delta t}$.
2. $L = \frac{200}{5.0 / 0.1} = \frac{200}{50} = \mathbf{4.0\text{ H}}$.
Numerical 10 Magnetic Energy Density in Solenoid
A long solenoid produces an axial magnetic field $B = 0.4\pi \times 10^{-2}\text{ T} \approx 0.01257\text{ T}$ inside its core. Calculate the magnetic energy density $u_B = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}$ in $\text{J/m}^3$. (Take $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7}\text{ T m/A}$).
1. $u_B = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0} = \frac{(4\pi \times 10^{-3})^2}{2 \times (4\pi \times 10^{-7})}$.
2. $u_B = \frac{16\pi^2 \times 10^{-6}}{8\pi \times 10^{-7}} = 2\pi \times 10 = 20\pi \approx \mathbf{62.83\text{ J/m}^3}$.

Topic 6: Mutual Inductance & Flux Linkage

Numerical 11 Mutual Induction Flux Linkage
A pair of adjacent coils has a mutual inductance of $1.5\text{ H}$. If the current in one coil changes from $0$ to $20\text{ A}$ in $0.5\text{ s}$, calculate the change of flux linkage with the other coil in Webers ($\text{Wb}$).
1. $N_2 \Phi_2 = M I_1 \implies \Delta(N_2 \Phi_2) = M \Delta I_1$.
2. $\Delta(N_2 \Phi_2) = 1.5\text{ H} \times 20\text{ A} = \mathbf{30\text{ Wb}}$.
Numerical 12 AC Generator Armature Peak Voltage
Kamla peddles a stationary bicycle attached to a $100\text{-turn}$ coil of area $0.10\text{ m}^2$. The coil rotates at $0.5\text{ rev/s}$ in a magnetic field $B = 0.01\text{ T}$ perpendicular to the axis. Calculate the maximum voltage generated $\varepsilon_0$ in Volts. (Use $\pi = 3.1416$).
1. $\varepsilon_0 = N B A (2\pi \nu)$.
2. $\varepsilon_0 = 100 \times 0.01 \times 0.10 \times 2 \times 3.1416 \times 0.5 = \mathbf{0.314\text{ V}}$.
