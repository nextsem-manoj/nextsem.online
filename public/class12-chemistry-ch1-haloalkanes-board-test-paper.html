Q.02 [NCERT Page 179] CBSE 2023, 2019, 2015 2 MARKS
Haloalkanes react with potassium cyanide (KCN) to yield alkyl cyanides (R—CN) as the major product, whereas with silver cyanide (AgCN) they produce alkyl isocyanides (R—NC). Give the scientific reason.
हैलोएल्केन पोटेशियम साइनाइड (KCN) के साथ अभिक्रिया करके मुख्य उत्पाद के रूप में एल्किल साइनाइड (R—CN) देते हैं, जबकि सिल्वर साइनाइड (AgCN) के साथ एल्किल आइसोसाइनाइड (R—NC) बनाते हैं। वैज्ञानिक कारण स्पष्ट कीजिए।
Reason: KCN is predominantly _____________, while AgCN is predominantly _____________. Bond formed: ____________
Solution & Ambident Nucleophile Principle Cyanide: Ambident Nucleophile
1. KCN is predominantly ionic: In solution, it dissociates into K+ and [ :C≡N: ]−. Both C and N can donate electrons, but the C—C bond is significantly stronger and more stable than the C—N bond, so attack occurs primarily via carbon → Alkyl cyanide (R—CN).
2. AgCN is predominantly covalent: The C—Ag bond is covalent, leaving only the unshared electron pair on Nitrogen free to attack the carbon of the alkyl halide → Alkyl isocyanide (R—NC).

(a) What is meant by an Asymmetric (Chiral) Carbon atom?
(b) Out of butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol, which one is chiral and optically active? Draw the structures of its enantiomers.
(क) असममित (किरल) कार्बन परमाणु से क्या तात्पर्य है?
(ख) ब्यूटेन-1-ऑल और ब्यूटेन-2-ऑल में से कौन सा किरल एवं प्रकाशिक सक्रिय (Optically Active) है? इसके प्रतिबिंबरूपों (Enantiomers) की संरचनाएं बनाइए।
(a) Chiral carbon definition: __________________________________; (b) Chiral molecule: ________________________
Solution & Stereochemistry C* bonded to 4 different groups
1. Asymmetric Carbon: An sp3 hybridised carbon atom bonded to four entirely different atoms or groups of atoms.
2. butan-2-ol is chiral: Its C2 is bonded to —H, —OH, —CH3, and —CH2CH3 (four different groups). It lacks plane of symmetry and exists as non-superimposable mirror images (enantiomers: (+) and (−) forms). In contrast, butan-1-ol has two identical —H atoms at C1 and is achiral.
Q.04 [NCERT Page 168–169] CBSE 2020, 2016 2 MARKS
Account for the following physical observations:
(a) Although haloalkanes are polar in nature, they are virtually insoluble in water.
(b) Arrange in increasing order of boiling points: bromomethane, bromoform, chloromethane, dibromomethane.
निम्नलिखित भौतिक प्रेक्षणों का वैज्ञानिक कारण दीजिए:
(क) यद्यपि हैलोएल्केन ध्रुवीय प्रकृति के होते हैं, फिर भी वे जल में लगभग अविलेय होते हैं।
(ख) क्वथनांक के बढ़ते क्रम में व्यवस्थित कीजिए: ब्रोमोमेथेन, ब्रोमोफॉर्म, क्लोरोमेथेन, डाइब्रोमोमेथेन।
(a) Reason for insolubility: ________________________________; (b) Order: ____________________________________
Solution & Intermolecular Forces H-bonding vs Van der Waals
1. Insolubility in water: To dissolve haloalkane in water, energy is needed to break intermolecular H-bonds between water molecules. The new dipole-dipole attractions set up between haloalkane and water are weaker than original hydrogen bonds, so energy released is insufficient → insoluble.
2. Boiling Point Order: Chloromethane < Bromomethane < Dibromomethane < Bromoform (van der Waals dispersion forces increase with molecular mass and size of halogen).
Q.05 [NCERT Page 169] CBSE 2017, 2014 2 MARKS
Explain why the dipole moment of chlorobenzene (μ = 1.69 D) is significantly lower than that of cyclohexyl chloride (μ = 1.86 D).
स्पष्ट कीजिए कि क्लोरोबेंजीन का द्विध्रुव आघूर्ण (μ = 1.69 D), साइक्लोहेक्सिल क्लोराइड (μ = 1.86 D) की तुलना में काफी कम क्यों होता है?
Reasons: 1. Hybridisation difference: ___________________________; 2. Resonance effect: ___________________________
Solution & C—Cl Polarity Analysis sp2 vs sp3 Carbon + Resonance
1. Hybridisation: In chlorobenzene, carbon is sp2 hybridised (33.3% s-character, more electronegative, releases electrons less readily to Cl). In cyclohexyl chloride, carbon is sp3 hybridised (25% s-character, less electronegative), making C—Cl bond more polar.
2. Resonance Effect: In chlorobenzene, the lone pair on chlorine enters into resonance with benzene ring, developing partial double bond character in C—Cl bond and shortening bond length (169 pm vs 177 pm). Since dipole moment = charge × distance, shorter length and resonance decrease dipole moment.

(क) क्लोरोफॉर्म (CHCl3) को गहरे रंग की बोतलों में मुंह तक भरकर तथा उसमें 1% एथेनॉल मिलाकर क्यों रखा जाता है?
(ख) वायु और प्रकाश के संपर्क में आने पर क्लोरोफॉर्म के ऑक्सीकरण की रासायनिक समीकरण लिखिए।
(a) Reason: __________________________________________________; (b) Equation: ____________________________________
Solution & Phosgene Neutralisation 2 CHCl3 + O2 → 2 COCl2 (Phosgene)

2. Why dark bottles & ethanol: Phosgene is an extremely poisonous gas. Dark amber bottles exclude light to prevent oxidation. 1% ethanol is added because it converts any traces of poisonous phosgene into harmless, non-toxic diethyl carbonate: COCl2 + 2 C2H5OH → (C2H5O)2CO + 2 HCl.

Q.08 [NCERT Page 173] CBSE 2022, 2019, 2017 4 MARKS
Write balanced chemical equations along with specific reaction conditions for the following Halogen Exchange Named Reactions:
(a) Finkelstein Reaction: Explain the role of dry acetone in driving the reaction forward.
(b) Swarts Reaction: Name two metallic fluorides used and state the main organic product when chloromethane is treated with silver fluoride.
निम्नलिखित हैलोजन विनिमय नाम अभिक्रियाओं (Named Reactions) के लिए संतुलित रासायनिक समीकरण एवं परिस्थितियां लिखिए:
(क) फिंकेलस्टाइन अभिक्रिया: अभिक्रिया को आगे बढ़ाने में शुष्क एसीटोन की भूमिका स्पष्ट कीजिए।
(ख) स्वार्ट्स अभिक्रिया: प्रयुक्त दो धातु फ्लोराइडों के नाम लिखिए तथा क्लोरोमेथेन की AgF के साथ अभिक्रिया का उत्पाद बताइए।
(a) Finkelstein: R—X + NaI dry acetone→ ____________________; Role of acetone: ____________________
(b) Swarts: R—Cl + AgF Δ→ ____________________
Solution & Halogen Exchange Reactions Finkelstein (Iodide) • Swarts (Fluoride)
1. Finkelstein Reaction:
R—Cl (or R—Br) + NaI dry acetone→ R—I (Alkyl iodide) + NaCl↓ (or NaBr↓)
Role of Dry Acetone: NaI is soluble in acetone, but NaCl and NaBr are practically insoluble in dry acetone and precipitate out. According to Le Chatelier's Principle, continuous precipitation of NaCl/NaBr removes product and shifts equilibrium forward.
2. Swarts Reaction:
CH3—Br + AgF Δ→ CH3—F (Fluoromethane) + AgBr↓
Metallic Fluorides: AgF, Hg2F2, CoF2, or SbF3.

(ग) कॉपर उत्प्रेरक के बिना बेंजीन डाइऐजोनियम क्लोराइड से आयोडोबेंजीन कैसे प्राप्त की जाती है?

Q.10 [NCERT Page 177–183] CBSE 2023, 2020, 2018 6 MARKS
(a) Differentiate between SN1 and SN2 mechanisms of nucleophilic substitution with respect to: (i) Kinetics / Order of reaction, (ii) Intermediate / Transition state, and (iii) Stereochemical outcome (Inversion vs Racemisation).
(b) Predict which compound in each of the following pairs reacts faster in SN2 displacement with reasons:
• (i) 1-Bromobutane or 2-Bromobutane
• (ii) 1-Bromobutane or 1-Iodobutane
(c) Why do allylic and benzylic halides show high reactivity towards SN1 reactions?
(क) नाभिकरागी प्रतिस्थापन की SN1 एवं SN2 क्रियाविधियों में निम्नलिखित आधारों पर अंतर स्पष्ट कीजिए: (i) बलगतिकी (अभिक्रिया की कोटि), (ii) मध्यवर्ती / संक्रमण अवस्था, (iii) त्रिविम रासायनिक परिणाम (प्रतिलोमन बनाम रेसिमीकरण)।
(ख) निम्नलिखित युग्मों में से SN2 अभिक्रिया में कौन अधिक तेजी से क्रिया करेगा और क्यों?
(ग) ऐलिलिक एवं बेन्जिलिक हैलाइड SN1 अभिक्रियाओं के प्रति उच्च क्रियाशीलता क्यों दर्शाते हैं?
(a) Table of SN1 vs SN2 differences: __________________________________________________
(b) Faster in SN2: (i) ____________________ (ii) ____________________; (c) Allylic/Benzylic: ____________________
Solution & Mechanism Comparative Analysis SN2: 1° > 2° > 3° • SN1: 3° > 2° > 1°
1. Comparison Table:
• Kinetics: SN2 is bimolecular (Rate = k[R—X][Nu−], 2nd order); SN1 is unimolecular (Rate = k[R—X], 1st order).
• Transition vs Intermediate: SN2 proceeds in single step via pentacoordinated transition state; SN1 proceeds in two steps via planar carbocation intermediate.
• Stereochemistry: SN2 leads to 100% Walden Inversion (backside attack); SN1 leads to Racemisation (attack possible from both faces of planar carbocation).
2. Faster in SN2:
• (i) 1-Bromobutane: Primary (1°) halide experiences less steric hindrance than secondary (2°) 2-bromobutane.
• (ii) 1-Iodobutane: C—I bond is weaker and iodide (I−) is a far better leaving group than bromide (Br−).
3. Allylic & Benzylic High SN1 Reactivity: The intermediate carbocations (allyl C+ and benzyl C+) are stabilized by resonance with the adjacent π-bond / aromatic ring.
Q.011 [NCERT Page 185–187] CBSE 2023, 2019, 2017 6 MARKS
(a) State four distinct reasons why aryl halides (like chlorobenzene) are extremely unreactive towards nucleophilic substitution reactions.
(b) What is Dow's Process? Write the balanced equation with specific industrial conditions (temperature and pressure).
(c) Explain why the presence of nitro (—NO2) group at ortho- and para-positions drastically accelerates the rate of nucleophilic substitution, while at meta-position it shows no significant activating effect.
(क) चार स्पष्ट कारण बताइए कि एरिल हैलाइड (जैसे क्लोरोबेंजीन) नाभिकरागी प्रतिस्थापन अभिक्रियाओं के प्रति अत्यधिक अक्रिय क्यों होते हैं?
(ख) डाउ प्रक्रम (Dow's Process) क्या है? विशिष्ट औद्योगिक परिस्थितियों (ताप व दाब) सहित समीकरण लिखिए।
(ग) स्पष्ट कीजिए कि ऑर्थो एवं पैरा स्थितियों पर नाइट्रो (—NO2) समूह की उपस्थिति नाभिकरागी प्रतिस्थापन की दर को अत्यधिक क्यों बढ़ा देती है, जबकि मेटा स्थिति पर इसका कोई विशेष प्रभाव नहीं पड़ता?
(a) 4 Reasons: 1. Resonance 2. Hybridisation 3. Phenyl cation 4. Repulsion
(b) Dow's Process: C6H5Cl + NaOH 623 K, 300 atm→ ____________________
(c) Resonance stabilization of carbanion at o/p: ____________________
Solution & Aryl Halide Reactivity Resonance + sp2 Carbon + Meisenheimer Complex
1. 4 Reasons for Low Reactivity:
• Resonance Effect: C—Cl bond acquires partial double bond character, becoming shorter (169 pm) and harder to cleave.
• Hybridisation of Carbon: Carbon is sp2 hybridised (more electronegative), holds electron pair tightly.
• Instability of Phenyl Cation: Self-ionisation to phenyl cation does not occur due to high instability.
• Electronic Repulsion: Electron-rich benzene ring repels incoming nucleophile.
2. Dow's Process:
C6H5Cl + NaOH 623 K, 300 atm→ C6H5O−Na+ H+→ C6H5OH (Phenol) + NaCl
3. Activating Effect of —NO2 at o/p-positions: The nucleophilic attack forms a resonance-stabilised carbanion intermediate (Meisenheimer complex). When —NO2 is at ortho or para, one of the resonating structures places the negative charge directly on the carbon bearing the strongly electron-withdrawing —NO2 group, stabilizing the carbanion. At meta, the negative charge never falls on the carbon bearing —NO2, so no direct resonance stabilization occurs.

(a) Saytzeff Rule: ____________________________________; Major: ____________; Minor: ____________

Solution & Elimination / Organometallics Saytzeff Elimination • Grignard Reagent
1. Saytzeff Rule: In dehydrohalogenation (β-elimination), the preferred product is that alkene which has greater number of alkyl groups attached to the doubly bonded carbon atoms (more substituted alkene).
• CH3—CH2—CH2—CH(Br)—CH3 + alc. KOH Δ→ CH3—CH2—CH=CH—CH3 (Pent-2-ene, 81% Major) + CH3—CH2—CH2—CH=CH2 (Pent-1-ene, 19% Minor) + KBr + H2O.
2. Grignard Reagent Preparation:
CH3CH2Br + Mg dry ether→ CH3CH2MgBr (Ethylmagnesium bromide)
3. Why Anhydrous Conditions are Compulsory: Grignard reagents are highly reactive bases and react violently with any source of active hydrogen (water, alcohols, amines) to form alkanes:
R—MgX + H—OH → R—H (Alkane) + Mg(OH)X↓

Q.13 [NCERT Page 170–189] CBSE 2023, 2020, 2019, 2018 8 MARKS (2M × 4)
Convert the following organic compounds in not more than two steps, writing all intermediate reagents:
• (a) Propene → Propan-1-ol
• (b) Benzene → Biphenyl (Diphenyl)
• (c) 1-Bromopropane → 2-Bromopropane
• (d) Toluene → Benzyl alcohol
सभी माध्यमिक अभिकर्मकों का उल्लेख करते हुए अधिकतम दो पदों में निम्नलिखित कार्बनिक परिवर्तनों को पूर्ण कीजिए:
• (क) प्रोपीन → प्रोपेन-1-ऑल

• (घ) टॉलूईन → बेन्जिल ऐल्कोहॉल

Solution & Synthesis Pathways Standard 2-Step CBSE Conversions
1. (a) Propene → Propan-1-ol:
CH3—CH=CH2 + HBr benzoyl peroxide→ CH3CH2CH2Br (1-Bromopropane) aq. KOH→ CH3CH2CH2OH (Propan-1-ol) + KBr.
2. (b) Benzene → Biphenyl:
C6H6 + Cl2 anhy. FeCl3→ C6H5Cl (Chlorobenzene) 2 Na / dry ether, Δ (Fittig rxn)→ C6H5—C6H5 (Biphenyl) + 2 NaCl.
3. (c) 1-Bromopropane → 2-Bromopropane:
CH3CH2CH2Br alc. KOH, Δ→ CH3—CH=CH2 (Propene) HBr (Markovnikov)→ CH3—CH(Br)—CH3 (2-Bromopropane).
4. (d) Toluene → Benzyl alcohol:
C6H5CH3 + Cl2 Cl2 / hν (light)→ C6H5CH2Cl (Benzyl chloride) aq. KOH→ C6H5CH2OH (Benzyl alcohol) + KCl.

ESSENTIAL ORGANIC REAGENTS CHEATSHEET:
• Thionyl Chloride (SOCl2): Converts R—OH to pure R—Cl (SO2↑, HCl↑ gas).
• NaI / Dry Acetone (Finkelstein): Halogen exchange to form R—I.
• AgF, Hg2F2, CoF2, SbF3 (Swarts): Prepares fluoroalkanes (R—F).
• Alcoholic KOH + Δ: Dehydrohalogenation (β-elimination / Saytzeff).
• Aqueous KOH: Nucleophilic substitution to form alcohols (R—OH).
• KCN: Forms R—CN (cyanides); AgCN: Forms R—NC (isocyanides).
• Mg / Dry Ether: Forms Grignard reagent (R—MgX).
• 2 Na / Dry Ether: Wurtz (R—R), Wurtz-Fittig (Ar—R), Fittig (Ar—Ar).
