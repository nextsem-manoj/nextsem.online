Quantitative Chemical Laws & Dalton's Atomic Theory

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Law of Conservation of Mass (Lavoisier, 1774): In any closed chemical reaction, $\sum \text{Mass of Reactants} = \sum \text{Mass of Products}$. E.g., $100\text{ g }\text{CaCO}_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 56\text{ g }\text{CaO} + 44\text{ g }\text{CO}_2$.
  • Law of Constant Proportions (Proust, 1799): A pure chemical compound always contains its elements combined together in fixed mass proportions, regardless of source. E.g., Pure $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ has $m_{\text{H}} : m_{\text{O}} = 1 : 8$; $\text{NH}_3$ has $m_{\text{N}} : m_{\text{H}} = 14 : 3$.
  • Dalton's Atomic Postulates (1808):
    • Matter is composed of indestructible, indivisible particles called atoms.
    • Atoms of a given element are identical in mass and chemical properties.
    • Atoms of different elements have different masses and chemical properties.
  • Postulates Disproved by Modern Physics:
    • "Atoms are indivisible" $\rightarrow$ Disproved by subatomic particles ($e^-, p, n$).
    • "Atoms of same element have identical mass" $\rightarrow$ Disproved by Isotopes (e.g. $^{35}\text{Cl}$ and $^{37}\text{Cl}$).
    • "Atoms of different elements have different masses" $\rightarrow$ Disproved by Isobars (e.g. $^{40}_{18}\text{Ar}$ and $^{40}_{20}\text{Ca}$).

Charge-to-Mass Discoveries: Electron & Canal Rays

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Cathode Rays (Electrons): High voltage applied across low pressure gas ($10^{-2}\text{ mm Hg}$) emits invisible rays from cathode toward anode.
    • Ratio $\frac{e}{m_e} = 1.76 \times 10^{11}\text{ C/kg}$ is a universal constant (independent of cathode metal and gas).
    • Electron rest mass $m_e = 9.109 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg} = \frac{1}{1837}\text{ u}$. Absolute charge $e = -1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$.
  • Canal Rays (Protons): Eugen Goldstein (1886) used a perforated cathode and observed positive rays traveling in the opposite direction.
    • Unlike cathode rays, the $\frac{e}{m}$ of canal rays depends on the nature of gas.
    • Maximum $\frac{e}{m}$ is obtained with Hydrogen gas (gives lightest positive particle: the Proton).
    • Proton mass $m_p = 1.6726 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg} \approx 1.00727\text{ u}$. Charge $= +1.602 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$.

Thomson's Atomic Model & Electrostatic Neutrality

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Thomson's Model (1898): Atom visualized as a sphere of radius $\approx 10^{-10}\text{ m}$ of uniform positive charge with electrons embedded to achieve electrostatic equilibrium.
  • Mass Distribution Hypothesis: Thomson assumed mass was uniformly distributed over the entire atomic volume.
  • Theoretical Alpha-Particle Prediction: Because positive charge and mass were thought to be diffuse, high-energy alpha particles ($\text{KE} \approx 5.5\text{ MeV}$) were predicted to pass through with miniscule deflections ($\theta < 1^\circ$).
  • Fatal Flaw: Could not account for the strong Coulombic repulsion that caused 1 in 12,000 alpha particles to experience back-scattering ($\theta \ge 90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$).

Rutherford's Gold Foil Alpha Scattering Dynamics

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Alpha Particle Parameters: $\text{He}^{2+}$ nucleus ($2p + 2n$, $q = +2e$, $m = 4\text{ u} \approx 6.64 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$), kinetic energy $\approx 5.5\text{ MeV}$.
  • Gold Foil Specifications: Thickness $t \approx 100\text{ nm} \approx 1000\text{ atoms}$, Atomic number $Z = 79$ (large nuclear charge produces substantial Coulomb repulsion).
  • Scattering Formula: Number of scattered particles $N(\theta) \propto \frac{1}{\sin^4(\theta/2)}$.
    • As scattering angle $\theta$ increases from $0^\circ$ to $180^\circ$, $N(\theta)$ drops drastically.
  • Distance of Closest Approach ($r_0$): At distance $r_0$ in head-on collision ($\theta = 180^\circ$), $\text{KE} = \text{PE} \implies \frac{1}{2}m v^2 = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}\frac{(2e)(Ze)}{r_0}$.
    • This established the upper bound for nuclear radius as $r_0 \approx 10^{-14}\text{ m} - 10^{-15}\text{ m}$.

Nuclear Density & The Electrodynamic Stability Paradox

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Spatial Dimensions Comparison:
    • Nuclear Radius $R_{\text{nuc}} \approx 10^{-15}\text{ m} = 1\text{ fm}$ (Fermi).
    • Atomic Radius $R_{\text{atom}} \approx 10^{-10}\text{ m} = 1\text{ \AA}$.
    • $\frac{R_{\text{atom}}}{R_{\text{nuc}}} = 10^5 \implies$ Nuclear volume fraction is $\left(\frac{10^{-15}}{10^{-10}}\right)^3 = \mathbf{10^{-15}}$ of the atom.
  • Nuclear Density ($\rho_{\text{nuc}}$): Inconceivably high $\rho = \frac{M}{V} \approx \mathbf{2.3 \times 10^{17}\text{ kg/m}^3}$. (1 cubic centimeter of nuclear matter weighs $\approx 230\text{ million tonnes}$).
  • Electrodynamic Collapse Flaw (Maxwell-Larmor):
    • An orbiting electron experiences centripetal acceleration $a = \frac{v^2}{r}$.
    • According to classical electrodynamics, accelerating charges radiate electromagnetic energy continuously.
    • The electron would spiral inward and collapse into the nucleus in $\mathbf{\sim 10^{-8}\text{ seconds}}$, contradicting the physical reality of stable matter.

👁️ Check Solution2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³

👁️ Check Solution10⁻⁸ seconds (10 nanoseconds)

Bohr's Quantization & Chadwick's Neutron Nuclear Reaction

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Bohr's Revolutionary Postulates (1913):
    • Electrons revolve only in non-radiating stationary orbits where orbital angular momentum is an integral multiple of $\frac{h}{2\pi}$: L = mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi} \quad (n = 1, 2, 3...)
    • Energy radiation occurs exclusively upon quantum jumps between stationary states: \Delta E = E_2 - E_1 = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}
  • Discovery of the Neutron (1932):
    • James Chadwick bombarded a Beryllium target with $\alpha$-particles: ^9_4\text{Be} + ^4_2\text{He} \longrightarrow ^{12}_6\text{C} + ^1_0\text{n}
    • Neutron mass $m_n = 1.6749 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg} = 1.00866\text{ u}$ (slightly greater than proton $m_p = 1.6726 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$).
    • Charge $= 0$. Present in all atomic nuclei except ordinary hydrogen ($^1_1\text{H}$ / Protium).

Bohr-Bury Scheme: Multi-Shell Electronic Configurations

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • $2n^2$ Rule: Maximum capacity of shell $n$: $K(n=1)=2, L(n=2)=8, M(n=3)=18, N(n=4)=32, O(n=5)=50$.
  • Valence Shell Cap: The outermost orbit cannot contain more than 8 electrons regardless of shell capacity.
  • Penultimate Shell Cap: The shell immediately inside the outermost orbit cannot exceed 18 electrons.
  • Why Potassium ($Z=19$) is 2, 8, 8, 1 (Not 2, 8, 9): M-shell can theoretically hold 18, but once M-shell reaches 8 electrons, it acts as the outermost shell. To prevent exceeding the octet rule, the 19th electron enters the N-shell ($4s$).

Isoelectronic Species, Valence Shells & Ionic Valencies

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Valence Electrons: Number of electrons residing in the outermost shell.
  • Valency Formula:
    • For Metals (Valence $e^- \le 4$): $\text{Valency} = \text{Valence } e^-$ (e.g. $\text{Na}=1, \text{Mg}=2, \text{Al}=3, \text{Si}=4$).
    • For Non-Metals (Valence $e^- > 4$): $\text{Valency} = 8 - \text{Valence } e^-$ (e.g. $\text{N}: 8-5=3; \text{O}: 8-6=2; \text{F}: 8-7=1$).
  • 10-Electron Isoelectronic Series (Neon Core):
    • $\text{N}^{3-} (7+3=10), \text{O}^{2-} (8+2=10), \text{F}^- (9+1=10), \text{Ne} (10), \text{Na}^+ (11-1=10), \text{Mg}^{2+} (12-2=10), \text{Al}^{3+} (13-3=10)$.
  • 18-Electron Isoelectronic Series (Argon Core):
    • $\text{P}^{3-}, \text{S}^{2-}, \text{Cl}^-, \text{Ar}, \text{K}^+, \text{Ca}^{2+}$.

Nuclear Composition & Subatomic Particle Balance in Polyatomic Ions

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Atomic Number ($Z$): $Z = \text{number of nuclear protons } (p) = e^- \text{ (in neutral atom)}$.
  • Mass Number ($A$): $A = \text{Protons } (p) + \text{Neutrons } (n) \implies n = A - Z$.
  • Ions Particle Arithmetic:
    • Cation $^{A}_{Z}\text{X}^{m+}$: $p = Z, n = A - Z, e^- = Z - m$.
    • Anion $^{A}_{Z}\text{Y}^{k-}$: $p = Z, n = A - Z, e^- = Z + k$.
  • Polyatomic Ion Calculations:
    • Carbonate ($\text{CO}_3^{2-}$): $e^- = 6 + 3(8) + 2 = \mathbf{32\text{ electrons}}$, $p = 6 + 3(8) = \mathbf{30\text{ protons}}$.
    • Sulfate ($\text{SO}_4^{2-}$): $e^- = 16 + 4(8) + 2 = \mathbf{50\text{ electrons}}$, $p = 16 + 4(8) = \mathbf{48\text{ protons}}$.
    • Ammonium ($\text{NH}_4^+$): $e^- = 7 + 4(1) - 1 = \mathbf{10\text{ electrons}}$, $p = 7 + 4(1) = \mathbf{11\text{ protons}}$.

Isotopic Abundance Mathematics, Isobars & Nuclear Medicine

📘 Core Concept Notes (मुख्य अवधारणाएँ)

  • Isotopes: Same atomic number ($Z$), different mass number ($A$).
    • Chemical properties are identical because electronic configurations are the same.
    • Physical properties differ due to differences in nuclear mass.
  • Average Atomic Mass Mathematics:
    • Chlorine: $\bar{A} = \left(35 \times \frac{75}{100}\right) + \left(37 \times \frac{25}{100}\right) = 26.25 + 9.25 = \mathbf{35.5\text{ u}}$.
    • Boron: If average mass is $10.8\text{ u}$ with $^{10}\text{B}$ and $^{11}\text{B}$, then $\%^{10}\text{B} = \mathbf{20\%}$ and $\%^{11}\text{B} = \mathbf{80\%}$.
  • Isotopes in Medicine & Industry:
    • Cobalt-60 ($^{60}\text{Co}$): Radiotherapy treatment for localized cancer tumors.
    • Iodine-131 ($^{131}\text{I}$): Diagnostic scan and radiation therapy for goitre/thyroid disorders.
    • Sodium-24 ($^{24}\text{Na}$): Tracer for tracking cardiovascular blood flow leaks.
    • Carbon-14 ($^{14}\text{C}$): Radiocarbon dating of fossils (half-life $t_{1/2} = 5730\text{ years}$).
    • Uranium-235 ($^{235}\text{U}$): Fissile fuel in nuclear reactors.
  • Isobars: Atoms of different elements having different atomic numbers ($Z$) but the same mass number ($A$). E.g. $^{40}_{18}\text{Ar}$ ($p=18, n=22$) and $^{40}_{20}\text{Ca}$ ($p=20, n=20$). Chemical properties are completely different!
