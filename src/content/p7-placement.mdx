# Phase 7: Placement Preparation

Building skills is only 50% of the journey. The other 50% is marketing those skills and passing the interview process. In this final phase, we focus entirely on getting you hired.

---

## 1. Resume Building

Your resume is your ticket to getting an interview. Most tech resumes are read by automated Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) before a human ever sees them.

**Best Practices:**
- Keep it to exactly **One Page**.
- Do not include a photo or colorful graphics (ATS systems hate them).
- Highlight your **Projects** above your education (unless you are a recent grad from a top-tier college).
- Use Action Verbs: *“Built a React dashboard...”* instead of *“Was responsible for a React dashboard...”*

---

## 2. Optimizing Your LinkedIn Profile

Recruiters actively search for developers on LinkedIn. Make sure they find you.

- **Headline:** Be specific. *"Frontend Developer | React & Node.js"* instead of *"Student at IGNOU"*.
- **About Section:** Tell a short story about your coding journey and what you are passionate about.
- **Projects:** Link your live Capstone projects directly in the Featured section.
- **Networking:** Connect with HRs, tech recruiters, and senior developers at companies you want to work for.

---

## 3. The GitHub Portfolio

Your GitHub is your real-world resume.
- Pin your top 4 projects to your profile.
- Ensure every pinned project has a professional `README.md` with a live demo link.
- Keep a consistent "green commit graph" by coding and pushing regularly.

---

## 4. Technical Interview Questions

Tech interviews usually consist of multiple rounds. We will prepare you for the most common questions.

**Common Frontend Questions:**
- *What is the difference between `var`, `let`, and `const`?*
- *Explain the CSS Box Model.*
- *What are React Hooks and why do we use them?*
- *Explain the Virtual DOM in React.*
- *What is Closure in JavaScript?*

---

## 5. Data Structures & Algorithms (DSA) Basics

While frontend roles focus less on complex DSA than backend roles, you still need to know the basics to pass coding assessments.

- Arrays & Strings manipulation.
- Basic Sorting and Searching algorithms.
- Time and Space Complexity (Big O Notation).

---

## 6. Mock Interviews & Communication

The best way to get good at interviews is to practice them.

- Participate in peer mock interviews.
- Practice the **STAR Method** (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for behavioral questions (e.g., *"Tell me about a time you faced a difficult bug."*)
- Improve your English communication skills. Speak clearly, confidently, and honestly (if you don't know an answer, say "I don't know, but here is how I would find out.")

---
