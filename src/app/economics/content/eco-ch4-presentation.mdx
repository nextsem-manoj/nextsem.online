# Chapter 4: Presentation of Data

## 📊 Three Modes of Presentation

Once data is organized, we present it in ways that make comparison instant and visual. The three main methods are:

### 📝 1. Textual Presentation
* **What is it?** Data is described directly inside paragraphs of text.
* **Best for:** Very small datasets (e.g., "In 2025, crop yields rose by 4%").

### 📋 2. Tabular Presentation (Tables)
* **What is it?** Systematically arranging data in rows and columns.
* **Key Parts:** Table Number, Title, Captions (column headings), Stubs (row headings), and Source.

### 🎨 3. Diagrammatic / Graphic Presentation
* **What is it?** Visual representations like Bar Charts, Pie Charts, and Histograms.
* **Best for:** Spotting trends and comparisons instantly at a glance.

---

## 🍕 Bar Charts vs Pie Diagrams

Visual diagrams are highly popular in business and news reports:

* **📊 Bar Diagrams:** Rectangular blocks representing frequencies. Bars can be single, multiple (comparing two groups side-by-side), or component (stacking sub-parts inside a single bar).
* **🍕 Pie Diagrams (Circle Charts):** A circle divided into sectors. The total angle of a circle is **360°**, so categories are converted into degrees:
  * **Formula:**
  ```text
  Degree = (Component Value / Total Value) * 360
  ```

## 🏆 Concept Check

### ❓ Quick Question:
If a category represents 25% of the total value in a dataset, what will its sector angle be when drawing a Pie Diagram?

* **[A]** 25°
* **[B]** 90°
* **[C]** 180°
* **[D]** 360°

**Correct Answer:** **B** (90° - since $25\%$ of a $360°$ circle is $\frac{25}{100} \times 360 = 90°$).
