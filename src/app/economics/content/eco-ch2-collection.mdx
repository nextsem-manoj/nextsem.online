# Chapter 2: Collection of Data

## 📂 Primary vs Secondary Data

Data is the raw material of statistics. Depending on its source, data is classified into two types:

### 🔵 1. Primary Data
* **Definition:** Data collected by the investigator himself/herself for the first time for a specific purpose.
* **Source:** Direct interviews, surveys, questionnaires.
* **Features:** Highly accurate and reliable, but expensive and time-consuming to collect.

### 🟢 2. Secondary Data
* **Definition:** Data that has already been collected by someone else and is being reused.
* **Source:** Government reports, websites, newspapers, research publications.
* **Features:** Easy and cheap to get, but needs careful checking for bias and outdated details.

---

## 🔬 Census vs Sampling

When investigating a large group (called the **Population**), economists use two main survey methods:

| Method | Approach | Pros | Cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **🗳️ Census Survey** | Collecting data from *every single member* of the population. | 100% accurate; covers every detail. | Extremely expensive; takes years (e.g. Population Census). |
| **🔬 Sample Survey** | Collecting data from a *small representative group* (sample) of the population. | Cheap, fast, and highly practical. | Possible margin of error if the sample is biased. |

## 🏆 Concept Check

### ❓ Quick Question:
A researcher wants to study the monthly expenses of 10,000 college students in a city. Due to time limits, she surveys 150 students from different colleges. What is the population size and sample size of this study?

* **[A]** Population: 150, Sample: 10,000
* **[B]** Population: 10,000, Sample: 150
* **[C]** Population: 10,000, Sample: 10,000
* **[D]** Population: 150, Sample: 150

**Correct Answer:** **B** (Population represents the entire group under study [10,000], and Sample represents the selected subgroup [150]).
