# Chapter 1: Introduction

## 🍎 What is Economics?

Economics is the study of how society manages its **scarce resources** to satisfy unlimited human wants. 

At its core, economics revolves around three fundamental activities:
* **🛒 Consumption:** The act of using goods and services to satisfy wants (e.g., buying food or streaming videos).
* **🏭 Production:** The process of converting raw inputs into valuable goods and services (e.g., farming wheat or writing software).
* **🚚 Distribution:** How the total national income or goods produced are distributed among factors of production (land, labour, capital, enterprise).

---

## 📊 Why Do We Need Statistics?

Imagine trying to run a country without knowing its total population, unemployment rate, or average income. It would be impossible!

**Statistics** provides the concrete facts, numbers, and data that economists use to:
* 💡 **Understand Problems:** Know the exact scale of poverty, inflation, or job shortages.
* 🎯 **Formulate Policies:** Design target plans (e.g., free food schemes or tax breaks) based on real numbers.
* 📈 **Forecast Trends:** Predict future prices, supply demands, and population growth.

> 📊 **Key Quote:** "Without data, you are just another person with an opinion." — W. Edwards Deming

### ❓ Quick Question:
A bakery owner purchases flour, sugar, and yeast to bake fresh bread for sale in the market. Which economic activity does this purchase represent?

* **[A]** Consumption
* **[B]** Production
* **[C]** Distribution
* **[D]** Inflation

**Correct Answer:** **B** (Production - because inputs are being converted into output goods for sale).
