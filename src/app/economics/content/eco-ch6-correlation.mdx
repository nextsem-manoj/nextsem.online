# Chapter 6: Correlation

## 🔗 What is Correlation?

Correlation is a statistical tool that measures the relationship between two variables. If a change in one variable causes a corresponding change in the other, the two variables are said to be correlated.

* **📈 Positive Correlation:** Both variables move in the *same* direction (e.g., as temperature rises, ice cream sales rise).
* **📉 Negative Correlation:** Variables move in *opposite* directions (e.g., as the price of apples rises, the quantity demanded falls).
* **⚪ Zero Correlation:** No relationship exists (e.g., your height and your exam score).

---

## 🧮 Measuring Correlation

Karl Pearson's coefficient of correlation (r) is the most widely used mathematical index. Its value always lies strictly between **-1 and +1**:

* **r = +1:** Perfect positive correlation.
* **r = -1:** Perfect negative correlation.
* **r = 0:** No linear correlation.

> ⚠️ **Important Note:** Correlation does *not* prove causation. Just because two variables move together doesn't mean one causes the other (e.g. shoe size and vocabulary both increase as children grow up).

## 🏆 Concept Check

### ❓ Quick Question:
An economist calculates the correlation coefficient between the price of oil and travel sales to be r = -0.85. How would you interpret this relationship?

* **[A]** Weak positive correlation
* **[B]** Strong negative correlation
* **[C]** Weak negative correlation
* **[D]** Perfect positive correlation

**Correct Answer:** **B** (Strong negative correlation - since the value is negative and very close to -1).
