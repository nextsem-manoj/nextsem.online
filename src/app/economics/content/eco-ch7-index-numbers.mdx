# Chapter 7: Index Numbers

## 🌡️ The Economic Barometer

An **Index Number** is a statistical measure designed to show changes in a variable or group of related variables with respect to time, geographical location, or other characteristics.

* **🏛️ Base Year:** The year of reference from which changes are measured. The base year's index value is always set to **100**.
* **📅 Current Year:** The year for which we want to find the price change index.

### 💰 CPI vs WPI
* **CPI (Consumer Price Index):** Measures change in retail prices of goods/services bought by consumers. (Also called the Cost of Living Index).
* **WPI (Wholesale Price Index):** Measures change in price of goods sold in bulk at wholesale markets.

---

## 🧮 Calculating Price Index

The simplest formula is the **Simple Aggregative Price Index** (P01):

```text
Price Index = (Sum of current year prices / Sum of base year prices) * 100
```

* **Sum of P1** = Sum of prices in the current year.
* **Sum of P0** = Sum of prices in the base year.

If the index for 2026 is **125** (with base year 2020 = 100), it means prices have risen by **25%** overall since 2020.

## 🏆 Concept Check

### ❓ Quick Question:
The price index for a basket of goods in 2026 is calculated to be 142. Compared to the base year (Index = 100), by what percentage have average prices increased?

* **[A]** 142%
* **[B]** 42%
* **[C]** 100%
* **[D]** 0.42%

**Correct Answer:** **B** (42% - since the base index is 100, the change is 142 - 100 = 42%).
