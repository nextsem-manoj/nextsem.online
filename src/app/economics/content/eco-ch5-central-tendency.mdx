# Chapter 5: Measures of Central Tendency

## 🎯 What is Central Tendency?

A **measure of central tendency** is a single summary value that attempts to describe a whole set of data by identifying the central position within that dataset.

The three most popular measures are:
1. **🧮 Arithmetic Mean (Average):** Sum of all values divided by the total count.
2. **⚖️ Median (Middle Value):** The value separating the higher half from the lower half of data when ordered.
3. **🔥 Mode (Most Frequent):** The value that appears most often in the dataset.

---

## 📐 Formulas and Calculations

Let's look at how to compute these for individual series:

### 1. Arithmetic Mean (Average)
```text
Mean = (Sum of all values) / (Total number of items)
```
Where the sum of values is divided by the total count.

### 2. Median
* Arrange data in ascending order.
* If N is odd: Median is the value of the ((N + 1) / 2)-th term.
* If N is even: Median is the average of the (N / 2)-th and ((N / 2) + 1)-th terms.

## 🏆 Concept Check

### ❓ Quick Question:
Find the Median of this dataset representing daily wheat prices (in dollars): `[12, 15, 8, 20, 14]`

* **[A]** 12
* **[B]** 14
* **[C]** 15
* **[D]** 8

**Correct Answer:** **B** (14 - first arrange in order: `[8, 12, 14, 15, 20]`. Since $N=5$ is odd, the middle term is the 3rd term, which is 14).
