### 📊 Question Bank Architecture (30 High-Yield MCQs • Medium to Hard Level)

#### 📐 Section A: Graphical Based MCQs (Questions 1 – 10)
Each question features an authentic, high-resolution SVG diagram embedded directly in the question card:
1. **Q1 [Ch 4 • Graphical] Multi-Tile Algebra Model:** $(2x + 3)(3x + 1)$ decomposed into $6$ tiles of $x^2$, $11$ tiles of $x$, and $3$ unit tiles; Total Area $= 6x^2 + 11x + 3$. *(Fig. 4.8, Page 78)*
2. **Q2 [Ch 4 • Graphical] Geometric Dissection Proof of $a^2 - b^2$:** Slicing an L-shaped region of $a^2 - b^2$ and rearranging into a rectangle of dimensions $(a + b) \times (a - b)$. *(Fig. 4.3, Page 73)*
3. **Q3 [Ch 4 • Graphical] 3D Volumetric Dissection of $(a + b)^3$:** Partitioning a solid cube into $8$ blocks ($1$ of $a^3$, $1$ of $b^3$, $3$ of $a^2b$, $3$ of $ab^2$). *(Fig. 4.9, Page 83)*
4. **Q4 [Ch 4 • Graphical] Area Model of $(a + b + c)^2$:** Shaded area of the 6 off-diagonal rectangular sub-compartments equals $2(ab + bc + ca)$. *(Fig. 4.4, Page 75)*
5. **Q5 [Ch 5 • Graphical] Obtuse Triangle Circumcircle & Exterior Circumcentre:** For $\triangle ABC$ with obtuse $\angle B = 120^\circ$, circumcentre $O$ lies outside the triangle, subtending minor central angle $\angle AOC = 120^\circ$ (reflex $240^\circ$). *(Theorem 1 & Fig. 5.6, Page 97)*
6. **Q6 [Ch 5 • Graphical] Inscribed Angles on Major vs Minor Arcs:** Central angle $\angle BOC = 130^\circ \implies$ major arc $\angle BAC = 65^\circ \implies$ minor arc cyclic angle $\angle BDC = 115^\circ$. *(Theorem 9 & Fig. 5.21, Page 104)*
7. **Q7 [Ch 5 • Graphical] Two Parallel Chords on the Same Side:** Chords of $24\text{ cm}$ and $10\text{ cm}$ separated by distance $7\text{ cm} \implies$ radius $r = 13\text{ cm}$ using Baudhāyana-Pythagoras theorem. *(Fig. 5.16 & Ex 18, Page 116)*
8. **Q8 [Ch 5 • Graphical] Cyclic Quadrilateral with Exterior Angle:** Side $CD$ extended to $E$ with exterior $\angle ADE = 82^\circ$ and $\angle BAC = 38^\circ \implies \angle BCA = 60^\circ$. *(Theorem 11 & Ex 21, Page 116)*
9. **Q9 [Ch 5 • Graphical] Shortest Chord Through Interior Point $P$:** The chord perpendicular to $OP$ ($AB \perp OP$) has the strictly minimum length among all chords through $P$. *(Fig. 5.14 & Ex 23, Page 116)*
10. **Q10 [Ch 5 • Graphical] Locus of Midpoints of Congruent Chords:** Chords of length $16\text{ cm}$ in circle of radius $10\text{ cm}$ have their midpoints tracing a concentric circle of radius $6\text{ cm}$. *(Theorem 6 & Ex 16, Page 116)*

#### 🔢 Section B: Numerical Problem-Solving MCQs (Questions 11 – 20)
11. **Q11 [Ch 4 • Numerical] Invariant Consecutive Squares:** $P(n) = (n-1)^2 + (n+1)^2 - 2n^2 = 2$ and $Q(n) = (n-1)^2 + (n+2)^2 - n^2 - (n+1)^2 = 4$ for all $n$ (evaluating at $n = 999$). *(Example 1, Page 69)*
12. **Q12 [Ch 4 • Numerical] Reciprocal Cubics:** If $x + 1/x = 10/3$, then $x^3 + 1/x^3 = (10/3)^3 - 3(10/3) = \frac{730}{27}$. *(Exercise 8, Page 90)*
13. **Q13 [Ch 4 • Numerical] Symmetric Multivariable Identity:** If $a+b+c = 5$ and $ab+bc+ca = 10$, then $a^3+b^3+c^3 - 3abc = 5(5 - 10) = -25$. *(Exercise 11, Page 90)*
14. **Q14 [Ch 4 • Numerical] Conditional Zero Cubics:** If $x + y = -4 \implies x + y + 4 = 0 \implies x^3 + y^3 - 12xy + 64 = 0$. *(Exercise 13(i), Page 90)*
15. **Q15 [Ch 4 • Numerical] Reciprocal Factor Roots:** Both $(x - 2)$ and $(x - 1/2)$ are factors of $px^2 + 5x + r \implies p = r = -2$, hence $p - r = 0$. *(Exercise 10, Page 90)*
16. **Q16 [Ch 4 • Numerical] Rational Expression Domain Restrictions:** Simplifying $\frac{4x^2+4x+1}{4x^2-1} \times \frac{2x-1}{2x+1} = 1$, with undefined domain points $x \in \{-\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}\}$. *(Section 4.8, Page 87)*
17. **Q17 [Ch 5 • Numerical] Parallel Chords on Opposite Sides:** Chords of $12\text{ cm}$ and $16\text{ cm}$ in circle of radius $10\text{ cm}$ on opposite sides $\implies d = 8 + 6 = 14\text{ cm}$. *(Section 5.6, Page 101)*
18. **Q18 [Ch 5 • Numerical] Cyclic Quadrilateral Area & Diameter:** Sides $5, 12, 12, 5$ with right angle $\angle B = 90^\circ \implies \text{Area} = 30 + 30 = 60\text{ sq units}$, circumdiameter $= 13\text{ units}$. *(Exercise 10, Page 115)*
19. **Q19 [Ch 5 • Numerical] Inscribed Regular Hexagon:** In circle of radius $r = 14\text{ cm}$, perimeter $= 84\text{ cm}$ and apothem distance $= 7\sqrt{3}\text{ cm}$. *(Exercise 19, Page 116)*
20. **Q20 [Ch 5 • Numerical] Cyclic Quadrilateral Angle Algebra:** $\angle P = (2x+10)^\circ, \angle R = (3x-20)^\circ \implies x = 38^\circ$; $\angle Q = (y+25)^\circ, \angle S = (2y-10)^\circ \implies y = 55^\circ$. Largest angle is $\angle S = 100^\circ$. *(Exercise 8, Page 115)*

#### 💡 Section C: Conceptual & Proof-Based Reasoning MCQs (Questions 21 – 30)
21. **Q21 [Ch 4 • Conceptual] Divisibility of $n^3 - n$:** Factorised as $(n-1)n(n+1)$, representing 3 consecutive integers containing at least one multiple of 2 and one multiple of 3; hence always divisible by $2 \times 3 = 6$. *(Exercise 12, Page 90)*
22. **Q22 [Ch 4 • Conceptual] Identity vs Conditional Equation:** An identity holds universally for all real values in the variable's domain, whereas a conditional equation holds only for discrete root values. *(Chapter Summary, Page 90)*
23. **Q23 [Ch 4 • Conceptual] Removable Discontinuities in Rational Equivalence:** $\frac{(x-3)(x+2)}{x-3} = x + 2$ strictly holds on $\mathbb{R} \setminus \{3\}$ because at $x = 3$ the original rational expression has a removable singularity (hole). *(Section 4.8, Page 87)*
24. **Q24 [Ch 4 • Conceptual] Factoring Cuboid Dimensions:** Volume $V(s) = 3ps^2 - 15ps + 12p = 3p(s - 1)(s - 4)$, giving physical dimensions $3p$, $(s - 1)$, and $(s - 4)$. *(Exercise 6(ii), Page 89)*
25. **Q25 [Ch 5 • Conceptual] Continuous Symmetries of a Circle:** A circle has infinitely many lines of reflective symmetry (any straight line through the centre), debunking finite interval misconceptions. *(Section 5.2, Page 95)*
26. **Q26 [Ch 5 • Conceptual] Collinear Points Circumcircle Impossibility:** The perpendicular bisectors of two segments on a straight line are parallel and never intersect, proving no circle can pass through three collinear points. *(Theorem 1, Page 96)*
27. **Q27 [Ch 5 • Conceptual] Inscribed Parallelogram Theorem:** Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal ($\angle A = \angle C$) and opposite angles in a cyclic quad sum to $180^\circ \implies 2\angle A = 180^\circ \implies \angle A = 90^\circ$ (forcing it to be a rectangle). *(Exercise 14, Page 115)*
28. **Q28 [Ch 5 • Conceptual] Concyclicity Proof by Contradiction:** If point $D$ lies inside the circumcircle, exterior angle $\angle ADB > \angle AEB = \angle ACB$, which directly contradicts the given hypothesis $\angle ADB = \angle ACB$. *(Theorem 10 & Fig. 5.27B, Page 108)*
29. **Q29 [Ch 5 • Conceptual] Theorem 8 Chord Distance Reciprocity:** In a circle, a strictly longer chord is strictly closer to the centre ($AB > DE \iff d_1 < d_2$). *(Theorem 8, Page 102)*
30. **Q30 [Ch 5 • Conceptual] Thales' Semicircle Theorem Derivation:** Using radii $OA=OB=OC=r$ to form two isosceles triangles with base angles $a$ and $b \implies a + b + (a + b) = 180^\circ \implies 2(a + b) = 180^\circ \implies \angle ACB = a + b = 90^\circ$. *(Fig. 5.30 & Ex 24, Page 116)*

  * Tech job portals & learning apps
* Advertisers with matching keywords bid higher for your audience than irrelevant advertisers (like auto insurance or real estate).

---

### 2. Google Account Personalization (Your Browsing Profile)
* **How it works:** Google serves **Personalized Ads** based on the Google Account and device cookies of the person viewing the app.
* If you frequently browse coding tutorials, tech documentation, developer tools, or specific apps, Google’s ad profile assigns you those interest segments.
* **How to verify:** Open the app on a friend's phone, another Google account, or in a fresh test profile—you will see a completely different variety of ads.

* During the first **24 to 48 hours**, Google's ad exchange is still profiling the traffic quality, geography, and retention of requests coming from that unit.

### 5. Blocking Controls & Category Settings

### 🎨 How Flutter Works: The Simple, Intuitive Guide

To truly understand Flutter, forget traditional web development (HTML, CSS, DOM) for a moment. 

The easiest way to understand Flutter is this: **Flutter works like a modern 2D video game engine (like Unity or Unreal), but built for apps!** 🎮✨

---

### 1. The Core Secret: "The Painter on a Blank Canvas" 🖌️

In traditional web apps or hybrid tools (like Capacitor/Cordova):
* The app opens a hidden mini web browser (**WebView**) inside the phone.
* It renders HTML tags (`<div>`, `<p>`, `<button>`) and styles them with CSS.
* Every touch event has to travel from native Android ➔ to the browser engine ➔ to JavaScript ➔ and back. This can cause sluggish scrolling and touch delay.

**How Flutter does it differently:**
* Flutter **does not use a browser** and **does not use HTML**.
* When Flutter starts on your phone, it asks Android for a single blank screen space called a **Surface / Canvas**.
* Flutter contains its own high-speed graphics engine (**Impeller / Skia** written in C++).
* **It paints every single pixel on the screen directly at 60 to 120 frames per second**, just like a video game engine draws characters and scenery!

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                     Your Flutter Code                   │
│               (Dart Widgets & Game State)               │
└────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                             │  Direct Ahead-Of-Time (AOT)
                             ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│            Flutter Engine (C++ / Impeller)              │
│       • Text Layout  • Gestures  • Animation Pipeline   │
└────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                             │  Paints Pixels
                             ▼
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│             Phone GPU / Screen (60-120 FPS)             │
│        (Exact same look on Samsung, Pixel & iPhone)     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> 💡 **Why this matters**: Because Flutter draws every pixel itself, a button looks and behaves **100% identically** on every phone, with zero layout glitches between different Android versions.

---

### 2. "Everything is a Widget" (The Lego Brick Concept) 🧱

In Flutter, you build your entire screen by stacking **Widgets** inside other Widgets—exactly like snapping Lego bricks together.

A screen is just a tree of Widgets:

```dart
Scaffold(                           // 🧱 1. The phone screen shell
  backgroundColor: Color(0xFF070B19),
  body: Column(                     // 🧱 2. Stacks items vertically
    children: [
      Text("Welcome Developer!"),  // 🧱 3. A text label
      Row(                          // 🧱 4. Stacks items horizontally
        children: [
          Icon(Icons.favorite),     // 🧱 5. Heart icon ❤️
          Text("5 Hearts Left"),    // 🧱 6. Energy counter
        ],
      ),
      AdBannerWidget(),             // 🧱 7. Your live AdMob banner!
    ],
  ),
)
```

There are only two main types of widgets you will ever work with:
1. **`StatelessWidget`** (Static Lego): A widget that never changes after it is drawn (e.g. an icon, a static title, an about page).
2. **`StatefulWidget`** (Living Lego): A widget that holds data that can change (e.g. your heart count, command simulator typing, active tab). When data changes, you call `setState()` and Flutter instantly repaints only that changed portion of the screen in under 16 milliseconds!

---

### 3. How Flutter Runs at Native Speed (Dart AOT) ⚡

Why did Google choose **Dart** for Flutter instead of JavaScript or Python?

* **In Development (Debug Mode)**: Dart uses a **JIT (Just-In-Time)** compiler. This enables **Hot Reload**—you change a line of code, press Save, and your app updates on your phone in under 0.5 seconds without losing where you were!
* **In Production (Release Mode - your `.aab` file)**: Dart compiles using **AOT (Ahead-Of-Time)** compilation directly into **ARM Machine Code** (the raw 0s and 1s that the phone's CPU understands natively).
* There is **no JavaScript interpreter**, no bridge, and no browser overhead. It runs as fast as native C++ or Kotlin.

---

### 4. How Flutter Talks to the Phone Hardware (Platform Channels) 🔌

What happens when Flutter needs something native from Android—like displaying a **Google AdMob Rewarded Video**, checking battery, or vibrating the phone?

Flutter uses **Platform Channels** (think of it like an ultra-fast private telephone line):

```
Flutter (Dart Code)                   Android OS (Native Java/Kotlin)
────────────────────                   ───────────────────────────────
AdService:                            AdMob SDK:
"Hey Android, please show     ══════> Displays full-screen video ad.
the Rewarded Video ad now!"            User finishes watching video.
                                      
AdService:                            AdMob SDK:
"Great! Give user +5 hearts"  <══════ "User watched the full video!
and rebuild UI."                      Here is your reward event."
```

1. Flutter sends a binary message across the channel to the Android layer.
2. Android executes the native command (e.g. Google Mobile Ads SDK).
3. Android sends the result back to Flutter.
4. Flutter repaints your hearts counter from `0` to `5` instantly.

### 🎯 Recommended Release Frequency: Practical Best Practices

The golden rule for mobile releases depends entirely on **which track** you are releasing to:

---

### 1. The Quick Summary

| Track | Max Recommended per Day | Ideal Frequency | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Production** (Live to all public users) | **At most 1 per day** | **1 to 2 times a week** | Needs Google manual review (takes hours or days), prevents user update fatigue. |
| **Internal Testing** (Only you & your team) | **Unlimited** (5–10+ a day) | Whenever needed | Bypasses Google human review, updates within minutes for fast developer testing. |

---

### 2. Why You Should Avoid Multiple Production Releases a Day ⚠️

1. **Google Play Review Queue Congestion**:
   * Every production `.aab` is reviewed by Google’s automated scanners and human reviewers (especially for personal developer accounts, which can take anywhere from a few hours up to 24–48 hours).
   * If you submit Release 36, and 2 hours later submit Release 37, Google’s review system can get backed up or put your account under extended manual review.

2. **User "Update Fatigue" & Bad Reviews**:
   * If users notice their phone downloading 3 or 4 app updates a day for the same app, they may disable auto-updates, complain about mobile data usage, or even uninstall the app.

3. **Crash & AdMob Monitoring Window**:
   * When you release an update with new AdMob ad units or features, you want to watch the **Vitals & Crashes** dashboard for at least 24 hours to verify:
     * Are crashes near 0%?
     * Are AdMob impressions loading smoothly?
     * Are users earning their hearts without glitches?

---

### 3. The 3 Exceptions: When a Same-Day Production Release is OK 🚨

You should only push a second release on the same day if:
1. **Critical Crash Fix**: An unexpected bug is crashing the app on startup.
2. **AdMob Policy Fix**: You accidentally violated an Ad placement policy and need to fix it immediately.
3. **Broken Authentication / API**: An external API or auth system broke for all users.

---

### 4. Golden Rule of Version Codes (`versionCode`)

Google Play requires **every single upload** to have a strictly higher `versionCode` than the previous one:

* Current release: `versionCode = 36` (`versionName = "3.6.0"`)
* Next release: `versionCode = 37` (`versionName = "3.6.1"` or `"3.7.0"`)
* Next release: `versionCode = 38` (`versionName = "3.6.2"` or `"3.8.0"`)

> 💡 **Best Developer Workflow**:
> 1. Do all your coding, testing, and debugging locally during the day using `npm run mobile:run` or `npm run mobile:apk`.
> 2. Once everything is tested and you are happy with the day's features, bump `versionCode` by 1.
> 3. Run `npm run mobile:bundle` once and upload **1 clean, polished release** to Production!

I performed a thorough static analysis and code audit across all Flutter screens, services, widgets, and Android configurations. Here are the specific bugs discovered and resolved:

### 1. 🐛 Negative Index Timing Crash in `DesktopSimulatorScreen` (CRITICAL)
* **The Bug**: In `_advanceStep()`, the success SnackBar referenced `desktopSimulatorSteps[widget.state.desktopStepIdx - 1]`. * **The Fix**: Captured `final currentIdx = widget.state.desktopStepIdx` synchronously before incrementing, and referenced `desktopSimulatorSteps[currentIdx].successMsg`.

### 2. 🐛 Unclamped Step Index Out-of-Bounds Crash (HIGH)
* **The Bug**: If a user completed the Desktop simulator in a previous session, `widget.state.desktopStepIdx` could be stored in preferences as `>= desktopSimulatorSteps.length`.
